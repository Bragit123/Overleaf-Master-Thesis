When doing higher-order \gls{qft} calculations, the goal is to compare results with data from experiments.
In \cref{ch:parton_level} we calculated the partonic cross section for slepton pair production $\frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2}$ at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}, but this is not the process we observe in experiments.
What we really want is the \emph{hadronic} cross section for slepton pair production in proton--proton collisions $\sigma(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X)$.
Luckily, it turns out that we can write the hadronic cross section in terms of the partonic one by making use of \acrfullpl{pdf} in what is known as factorization~\cite{Ellis:1996mzs}.
This is the topic of the current chapter.

We will start off by introducing \glspl{pdf}, and show how they relate the partonic and hadronic cross sections.
Following this, we will discuss how to simplify integrals over \glspl{pdf} in order to more efficiently integrate them numerically.
Lastly, we briefly introduce how collinear divergences can be absorbed into the \glspl{pdf} before writing down the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in terms of the partonic cross sections we found in \cref{ch:parton_level}, readying them for numerical integration in \cref{ch:results}.

\section{Parton Distribution Functions}
Let $P$ be the four-momentum of a proton involved in some proton--proton collision. We will assume that the collision energy is sufficiently high that the mass of the proton and its constituents are negligible. We choose our coordinate system such that
\begin{equation}
    P^\mu = (\abs{\vec{P}}, 0, 0, \abs{\vec{P}}).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, we assume that the parton involved in the hard scattering carries a momentum fraction $x$, and that it propagates collinearly to the proton, i.e.\ that any momentum transverse to $P$ is negligible. Denoting the parton's four-momentum by $p$, we have
\begin{equation}
    p^\mu = xP^\mu = (x\abs{\vec{P}}, 0, 0, \abs{\vec{P}}).
\end{equation}
We can define the \gls{pdf} $f_i(x)$ for parton $i$ such that $f_i(x)dx$ represents the number of partons of type $i$ in the proton with momentum fraction between $x$ and $x+dx$~\cite{Thomson:2013zua}.
The \glspl{pdf} are not calculable quantities, but are instead fitted with experiments. In particular, this means that they are not smooth functions, but mere data points. As we will soon see, we will have to integrate over \glspl{pdf}, which must therefore be done numerically.

For slepton pair production, we will consider two protons with four-momenta $P_1$ and $P_2$. The hard scattering then involves a parton $i$ with four-momentum $p_1=x_1P_1$ and a parton $j$ with four-momentum $p_2=x_2P_2$. The momenta $p_1$ and $p_2$ correspond to the quark-momenta encountered in \cref{ch:parton_level}.
We define the hadronic squared \gls{com} energy by $s=(P_1+P_2)^2$, which is related to $\shat$ by
\begin{equation}
    \shat = (p_1+p_2)^2 = 2p_1 \cdot p_2 = x_1 x_2 \cdot 2P_1\cdot P_2 = x_1 x_2 (P_1+P_2)^2 = x_1 x_2 s.
\end{equation}
% Similarly to $z=Q^2/\shat$, we define the ratio
% \begin{equation}
%     \tau = Q^2/s = x_1x_2 z.
% \end{equation}

We now have all the components needed to express the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in terms of the partonic one. It is given by~\cite{Ellis:1996mzs}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X) = \sum_{ij} \int_0^1 dx_1 \int_0^1 dx_2 \, \theta(\shat-Q^2) f_i(x_1)f_j(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2}(ij \to \slepton^*\slepton + X),
\end{equation}
where the heaviside step function $\theta(\shat-Q^2)$ is there to make sure that $\shat > Q^2$, as required by \cref{eq:Q2_limits}.
The step function can be removed by changing the integration limits. Defining the ratio $\tau \equiv Q^2/s$, we can write
\begin{equation}
    \theta(\shat-Q^2) = \theta\biggl(x_1 x_2 s - s \frac{Q^2}{s}\biggr) = \theta(x_1 x_2 - \tau)
    \; \Longrightarrow \;
    \begin{cases}
        \tau \leq x_1 \leq 1
        \\
        \frac{\tau}{x_1} \leq x_2 \leq 1
    \end{cases},
\end{equation}
and we can write the hadronic cross section as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadron_xsec_general}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X) = \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i(x_1) f_j(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2}(ij \to \slepton^*\slepton + X).
\end{equation}

The integrals over \glspl{pdf} have to be computed numerically, but there are steps we can take to simplify them analytically first. We will now consider this more closely.


\subsection{Integrating over Parton Distribution Functions}
Many of the terms appearing in the partonic cross sections in \cref{eq:summary_dsigma_nlo} are proportional to either the Dirac delta distribution $\delta(1-z)$ or a plus distribution.
These distributions can be used to simplify the \gls{pdf} integrals in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general}. We start by considering the case with the delta distribution.

First, note that our newly defined variable $\tau$ is related to $z$ by
\begin{equation}
    \tau = x_1 x_2 z.
\end{equation}
Let $g(x_1,x_2)$ be some function of the partons's momentum fractions. We then have
\begin{align}
    \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2)\delta(1-z) &= \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) \delta\biggl(1-\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
    % \nonumber \\
    % &= \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) \frac{x_1 x_2^2}{\tau} \delta\biggl(x_2 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr)
    \nonumber \\
    &= g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
    \label{eq:int_x2_delta}
\end{align}
where we have rewritten the delta distribution as
\begin{equation}
    \delta\biggl(1-\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr) = \frac{x_1 x_2^2}{\tau} \delta\biggl(x_2 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
\end{equation}
before integrating out $x_2$.

Now consider a similar integral to \cref{eq:int_x2_delta}, but with a plus distribution $f_+(z)$ instead of the delta distribution. We find
\begin{align}
    &\int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) f_+(z) = \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dz}{z} g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1 z}\biggr) f_+(z)
    \nonumber \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \int_0^1 \frac{dz}{z} \Biggl[ g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1 z}\biggr) - z g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \Biggr] f(z)
    - g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, f(z),
\end{align}
where we have made a substitution $x_2 \to z$ and used \cref{eq:plus_dist_int_x_to_1}.
We can then switch variables back to $x_2$ in the first integral, leaving us with
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    &\int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) f_+\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
    \\ &\hspace{3em}
    = \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} \Biggl[ g(x_1,x_2) - \frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2} g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \Biggr] f\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr) + g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) F_f(x_1),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    F_f(x_1) \equiv - \int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz f(z)
\end{equation}
can be solved analytically.
We have only come across two plus distributions in our calculations, and their $F$-functions are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    F_{\frac{1}{1-z}}(x_1) &\equiv -\int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, \frac{1}{1-z} = \ln\biggl(1 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
    \\
    F_{\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}}(x_1) &\equiv -\int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, \frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z} = \int_0^{\ln(1-\tau/x_1)} u \, du = \frac{1}{2} \ln^2\biggl(1 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}



\subsection{Renormalized Parton Distribution Functions}
The initial-state partonic cross section in \cref{eq:summary_dsigmai_nlo} still contains a collinear divergence that was not removed by neither renormalized Lagrangian parameters nor an inclusion of all relevant Feynman diagrams.
It turns out that the way to get rid of the collinear divergence is by renormalizing the \glspl{pdf}.
The idea is very similar to that of renormalized perturbation theory encountered in \cref{sec:renormalization}.
There we started by acknowleding that the fields and couplings appearing in the bare Lagrangian are not physically measurable.
The same goes for the \glspl{pdf} defined in the preceding section.

Consider the \emph{bare} (infinite) \gls{pdf} $f_i^0(x)$. We can define a \gls{pdf} renormalization $Z_{f_i}(x)$ such that
\begin{equation}
    f_i^0(x) = Z_{f_i}(x) f_i^R(x),
\end{equation}
where $f_i^R(x)$ is the \emph{renormalized} (finite) \gls{pdf} which we extract from experimental data.
Furthermore, we introduce \gls{pdf} \emph{counterterms} $\delta_{f_i}(x)$ such that
\begin{equation}
    Z_{f_i}(x) \equiv 1 + \delta_{f_i}(x).
\end{equation}
The counterterms then contain the infinite part of $Z_{f_i}(x)$, and can---just as for counterterms in the Lagrangian---be expanded in the renormalized couplings of the theory (starting at \gls{nlo}, hence they do not appear at \gls{lo}).

Using our renormalized \glspl{pdf}, we can write the hadronic cross section in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general} at \gls{nlo} as
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}
    &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i^0(x_1) f_j^0(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}}{dQ^2}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, Z_{f_i}(x_1)Z_{f_j}(x_2)f_i^R(x_1) f_j^R(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}}{dQ^2}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, \Bigl[1+\delta_{f_i}(x_1)\Bigr]\Bigl[1+\delta_{f_j}(x_2)\Bigr] f_i^R(x_1) f_j^R(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}}{dQ^2}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, \Biggl\{
            f_i^R(x_1) f_j^R(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}
            + f_i^R(x_1) f_j^R(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO}}}{dQ^2}
            \\ &\hspace{10em}
            + \Bigl[\delta_{f_i}(x_1) + \delta_{f_j}(x_2) \Bigr] f_i^R(x_1) f_j^R(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}
            % \\ &\hspace{14em}
        % \Biggr\} + \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{NNLO}).
        \Biggr\} + \mathcal{O}(\alpha^4).
    \end{split}
\end{align}
The counterterms $\delta_{f_i}$ and $\delta_{f_j}$ can then be chosen in such a way that they nicely remove any collinear divergences in $\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO}}$.
Moving forward, we will denote the renormalized \glspl{pdf} without the $R$ superscript.

It is worth noting that there is some freedom in how we choose the \gls{pdf} counterterms, just as there was for Lagrangian counterterms.
Consequently, the renormalized \glspl{pdf} also depend on the chosen renormalization scheme.
In this thesis we renormalize \glspl{pdf} in the \gls{msbar} scheme, since this is the one used by \texttt{LHAPDF}~\cite{Buckley:2014ana}---the \texttt{C++} library we will use to extract \glspl{pdf} in \cref{ch:results}.
Note also that the \glspl{pdf} depend on scale in the same way as the renormalized fields and couplings.
To separate the two scales, we will denote the \emph{factorization scale} associated with \gls{pdf} renormalization by $\mu_F$. When we have to make the distinction clear, we denote the renormalization scale encountered in \cref{sec:shifting_spacetime_dim} by $\mu_R$.



\section{The Hadronic Cross Section}
We are now ready to write down the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in a form that is fit for numerical evaluation.
We start by extracting the different distributions that appear in the partonic cross section, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parton_xsec_ws}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^c}{dQ^2} = \frac{\hat\sigma_B^c}{x_1 x_2} \biggl\{
        \wsoft_c(z)\delta(1-z) + \wrad_c(z) + \sum_{f} \wf_c(z) f_+(z)
    \biggr\},
\end{equation}
where the sum is over all plus distributions. Here we have also defined
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_B^c \equiv x_1 x_2 \sigma_0^c,
\end{equation}
where $c\in\{\mathrm{LO},i,f\}$ denotes the kind of contribution we are looking at (\gls{lo} contribution or initial- or final-state \gls{nlo} corrections), such that $\hat\sigma_0^{i/f}$ gives the initial- and final-state prefactors in \cref{eq:summary_sigma0i,eq:summary_sigma0f}

The initial- and final-state prefactors $\hat\sigma_0^{i/f}$ are equal when $\shat=Q^2$ (they can safely be set to $d=4$ dimensions at this point\footnote{We do have a collinear divergence left in the initial-state correction, but since the prefactor $\hat\sigma_0^i$ is common to the \gls{lo} correction, its $\mathcal{O}(\eps)$ terms will not become relevant when removing the divergence with renormalized \glspl{pdf}, which we will do shortly.}), and we can choose which of them we want to use for $\hat\sigma_0^{\mathrm{LO}}$.
We will set $\hat\sigma_0^{\mathrm{LO}} = \hat\sigma_0^i$, since this completely removes the $x_1$ and $x_2$ dependence in $\sigma_B^{\mathrm{LO}}$.
This means that $\sigma_B^{\mathrm{LO}}$ is the Born cross section with
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_B^{\mathrm{LO}} = \sigma_B^i = x_1 x_2 \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\hat{s} Q^5} \fqliab(Q^2)
    = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC s Q^5} \fqliab(Q^2),
\end{equation}
where we now have the hadronic squared \gls{com} energy $s$ in the denominator, rather than the partonic $\shat$---hence no dependence on parton momentum fractions.
For final-state corrections, however, the dependence remains in $\sigma_B^f$:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_B^f = x_1 x_2 \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\hat{s}^3 Q} \fqliab(\hat{s})
    = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC s \hat{s}^2 Q} \fqliab(\hat{s}).
\end{equation}

In this thesis the only hard scattering process we have considered is quark--anti-quark annihilation. This limits the partons we have to sum over to get the full hadronic cross section. Inserting \cref{eq:parton_xsec_ws} into \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general}, we have
% At the parton-level we have considered quark--anti-quark annihilation exclusively, so the partonic cross section of interest is $\hat\sigma^{i/f}_{q\widebar{q}} \equiv \hat\sigma^{i/f}(q\widebar{q} \to \slepton^*\slepton+X)$, where the superscript $i$ or $f$ denotes initial- and final-state corrections, respectively.
% Inserting \cref{eq:parton_xsec_ws} into \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general}, we have
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\sigma^c}{dQ^2}
    &= 2 \sum_q \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} f_q(x_1) f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) x_1x_2 \frac{d\hat\sigma^c_{q\widebar{q}}}{dQ^2}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= 2\sum_q \vastl\{
            \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} f_q(x_1) f_{\widebar{q}}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \hat\sigma_B^c \biggl[
                \wsoft_c(1) + \sum_f \wf_c(1) F_f(x_1)
            \biggr]
            \\ &\hspace{4em}
            + \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} f_q(x_1) \hat\sigma_B^c \Biggl[
                f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) \wrad_c\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
                \\ &\hspace{5em}
                + \sum_f \biggl\{
                    f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) \wf_c\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
                    - \frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2} f_{\widebar{q}}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \wf_c(1)
                \biggr\} f\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
            \Biggr]
        \vastr\},
        \label{eq:hadron_xsec_full_w}
    \end{split}
\end{align}
where the factor $2$ in front of the sum is there because we only sum over quarks (not anti-quarks).
To get the full $\mathrm{LO}+\mathrm{NLO}$ contribution to the cross section, we can simply sum over $c \in \{\mathrm{LO},i,f\}$.

% %     \\

% % \end{subequations}
% \begin{subequations}
% \begin{align}

%     \\

% \end{align}
% \end{subequations}
% The \gls{lo} contribution to the partonic cross section is\footnote{Here we use the prefactor $\hat\sigma_{Q^2}$ because $Q^2$ is independent of $x_1$ and $x_2$, and therefore simpler than $\hat\sigma_0^f$. Note, however, that for the \gls{lo} and every other term proportional to $\delta(1-z)$, this choice is arbitrary since $\shat=Q^2$.}
The \gls{lo} contribution to the partonic cross section is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2} = \frac{\hat\sigma_B^{\mathrm{LO}}}{x_1 x_2}\delta(1-z),
\end{equation}
which in the language of \cref{eq:parton_xsec_ws} gives only one $w$-function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadron_w_lo}
    \wsoft_{\mathrm{LO}}(z) = 1.
\end{equation}

The initial-state correction to the partonic cross section was found to be
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^i}{dQ^2} &= \frac{\hat\sigma^i_B}{x_1 x_2} \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
        \vastl\{
            \Biggl(\frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4\Biggr)\delta(1-z)
            - \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z}
            \\ &\hspace{3em}
            + 2(1+z^2) \plusdistln
            - \frac{1}{\eps}\Biggl[ (1+z^2)\plusdist + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z) \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
Using \cref{eq:summary_G_eps} we can write
\begin{equation}
    G(\eps) \, \frac{1}{\eps} = \biggl[ \frac{1}{\eps} + \ln(4\pi) - \gamma_E \biggr] + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2} + \mathcal{O}(\eps).
\end{equation}
The collinear divergence can be removed by the renormalized \glspl{pdf}, which in the \gls{msbar} scheme leaves only the $\ln(\mu^2/Q^2)$ term.
The initial-state $w$-functions after renormalizing the \glspl{pdf} are then
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:hadron_w_initial_state}
\begin{align}
    \wsoft_i(z) &= \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} \Biggl\{ \frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4 - \frac{3}{2} \ln\frac{\mu_F^2}{Q^2} \Biggr\},
    \\
    \wrad_i(z) &= -\frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z},
    \\
    w^{\frac{1}{1-z}}_i(z) &= -\frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} (1+z^2) \ln\frac{\mu_F^2}{Q^2},
    \\
    w^{\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}}_i(z) &= 2 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} (1+z^2).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^f}{dQ^2} &= \frac{\hat\sigma^f_B}{x_1 x_2} \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \vastl\{
            \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda}
            + \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} \plusdist I^r_1
            \\ &\qquad
            + \delta(1-z) \Biggl[
                \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
                - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
                    \ln(4\lambda)
                    + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2}
                \Biggr) I^r_1
                + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
            \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here there are no collinear divergences to worry about, so writing down the $w$-functions is straight-forward:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:hadron_w_final_state}
\begin{align}
    \wsoft_f(z) &= \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \Biggl[
        \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
        - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
            \ln(4\lambda)
            + \ln\frac{\mu_R^2}{Q^2}
        \Biggr) I^r_1
        + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
    \Biggr],
    \\
    \wrad_f(z) &= \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda},
    \\
    w^{\frac{1}{1-z}}_f(z) &= \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} I^r_1,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)$, $I_1^r$ and $I_2^r$ were defined in \cref{eq:summary_g,eq:summary_Ir1,eq:summary_Ir2}, and we remind the reader of the K\"allen function shorthands $\lambda \equiv \lambda(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)$ and $\lambda_z \equiv \lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)$.

\Cref{eq:hadron_w_lo,eq:hadron_w_initial_state,eq:hadron_w_final_state} can now be inserted into \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_full_w} and integrated over numerically to obtain the hadronic cross section.
