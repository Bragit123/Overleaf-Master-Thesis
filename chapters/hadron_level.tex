\chapter{Hadron Level Slepton Pair Production}\label{ch:hadron_level}

When performing higher-order \gls{qft} calculations, the goal is to compare results with data from experiments.
In \cref{ch:parton_level} we calculated the partonic cross section for slepton pair production at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}, but this is not the process we observe in experiments.
What we really want is the \emph{hadronic} cross section for slepton pair production in proton--proton collisions $\sigma(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X)$.
Luckily, it turns out that we can write the hadronic cross section in terms of the partonic one by making use of \glspl{pdf} using what is known as factorization~\cite{Ellis:1996mzs}.
This is the topic of the current chapter.

We will start off by introducing \glspl{pdf}, and show how they relate the partonic and hadronic cross sections.
Following this, we will discuss how to simplify integrals over \glspl{pdf} in order to more efficiently integrate them numerically.
Lastly, we briefly introduce how collinear divergences can be absorbed into the \glspl{pdf} before writing down the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in terms of the partonic cross sections we found in \cref{ch:parton_level}, readying them for numerical integration in \cref{ch:results}.



\section{Parton Distribution Functions}
Let $P$ be the four-momentum of a proton involved in some proton--proton collision. We will assume that the collision energy is sufficiently high that the mass of the proton and its constituents---the partons---are negligible. We choose our coordinate system such that
\begin{equation}
    P^\mu = (\abs{\vec{P}}, 0, 0, \abs{\vec{P}}).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, we assume that the parton involved in the hard scattering carries a fraction $x$ of the proton's momentum, and that it propagates collinearly with the proton, i.e.\ that any momentum transverse to $P$ is negligible. Denoting the parton's four-momentum by $p$, we have
\begin{equation}
    p^\mu = xP^\mu = (x\abs{\vec{P}}, 0, 0, x\abs{\vec{P}}).
\end{equation}

We can define the \acrlong{pdf} $f_i(x)$ for parton $i$ such that $f_i(x)dx$ represents the number of partons of type $i$ in the proton with momentum fraction between $x$ and $x+dx$~\cite{Thomson:2013zua}.\footnote{Although similar, this is not a probability density function. The \glspl{pdf} can take on negative values, and they do not in general integrate to unity.}
The \glspl{pdf} are not perturbatively calculable quantities, but are instead fitted with experiments. As we will soon see, we will have to integrate over \glspl{pdf}, which must therefore be done numerically.

For slepton pair production, we will consider two protons with four-momenta $P_1$ and $P_2$.
The hard scattering then involves a parton of type $i$ with four-momentum $p_1=x_1P_1$ and a parton of type $j$ with four-momentum $p_2=x_2P_2$. The momenta $p_1$ and $p_2$ are then the same as the quark-momenta $p_1$ and $p_2$ encountered in \cref{ch:parton_level}.
We define the hadronic squared \gls{com} energy by $s=(P_1+P_2)^2$, which is related to the partonic $\shat$ by
\begin{equation}
    \shat = (p_1+p_2)^2 = 2p_1 \cdot p_2 = x_1 x_2 \cdot 2P_1\cdot P_2 = x_1 x_2 (P_1+P_2)^2 = x_1 x_2 s.
\end{equation}

We now have all the components needed to express the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in terms of the partonic one. It is given by~\cite{Ellis:1996mzs}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X) = \sum_{ij} \int_0^1 dx_1 \int_0^1 dx_2 \, \theta(\shat-Q^2) f_i(x_1)f_j(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2}(ij \to \slepton^*\slepton + X),
\end{equation}
where $\theta(\shat-Q^2)$ is the Heaviside step function, and is there to make sure that $\shat > Q^2$, as required by \cref{eq:Q2_limits}.
The step function can be removed by changing the integration limits. Defining the ratio $\tau \equiv Q^2/s$, we can write
\begin{equation}
    \theta(\shat-Q^2) = \theta\biggl(x_1 x_2 s - s \frac{Q^2}{s}\biggr) = \theta(x_1 x_2 - \tau),
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to setting the integration limits
\begin{equation}
        \tau \leq x_1 \leq 1, \qquad
        \frac{\tau}{x_1} \leq x_2 \leq 1.
\end{equation}
We can then write the hadronic cross section as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadron_xsec_general}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}(pp \to \slepton^*\slepton + X) = \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i(x_1) f_j(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2}(ij \to \slepton^*\slepton + X).
\end{equation}

The integrals over \glspl{pdf} have to be computed numerically, but there are steps we can take to simplify them analytically first. We will now consider this more closely.


\subsection{Integrating over Parton Distribution Functions}
Many of the terms appearing in the partonic cross sections in \cref{eq:summary_dsigma_nlo} are proportional to either the Dirac delta distribution $\delta(1-z)$ or a plus distribution.
These distributions can be used to simplify the \gls{pdf} integrals in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general}. We start by considering the case with the delta distribution.

First, note that our newly defined variable $\tau$ is related to $z$ by
\begin{equation}
    \tau = x_1 x_2 z.
\end{equation}
Let $g(x_1,x_2)$ be some function of the partons' momentum fractions. We then have
\begin{align}
    \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2)\delta(1-z) &= \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) \delta\biggl(1-\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
    \nonumber \\
    &= g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
    \label{eq:int_x2_delta}
\end{align}
where we have rewritten the delta distribution as
\begin{equation}
    \delta\biggl(1-\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr) = \frac{x_1 x_2^2}{\tau} \delta\biggl(x_2 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
\end{equation}
before integrating out $x_2$.

Now consider a similar integral to \cref{eq:int_x2_delta}, but with a plus distribution $f_+(z)$ instead of the delta distribution. We find
\begin{align}
    &\int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) f_+(z) = \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dz}{z} g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1 z}\biggr) f_+(z)
    \nonumber \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dz}{z} \Biggl[ g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1 z}\biggr) - z g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \Biggr] f(z)
    - g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, f(z),
\end{align}
where we have made a substitution $x_2 \to z$ and used \cref{eq:plus_dist_int_x_to_1}.
We can then switch variables back to $x_2$ in the first integral, leaving us with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:int_x2_plus}
\begin{split}
    &\int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} g(x_1,x_2) f_+(z)
    \\ &\hspace{3em}
    = \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} \Biggl[ g(x_1,x_2) - \frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2} g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \Biggr] f\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr) + g\biggl(x_1, \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) F_f(x_1),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    F_f(x_1) \equiv - \int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz f(z).
\end{equation}
The function $F_f(x_1)$ can then be solved analytically. We have only come across two plus distributions in our calculations, and their $F$-functions are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    F_{\frac{1}{1-z}}(x_1) &\equiv -\int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, \frac{1}{1-z} = \ln\biggl(1 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr),
    \\
    F_{\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}}(x_1) &\equiv -\int_0^{\tau/x_1} dz \, \frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z} = \int_0^{\ln(1-\tau/x_1)} u \, du = \frac{1}{2} \ln^2\biggl(1 - \frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}




\subsection{Renormalized Parton Distribution Functions}
The initial-state partonic cross section in \cref{eq:summary_dsigmai_nlo} still contains a collinear divergence that was not removed by neither renormalized Lagrangian parameters nor an inclusion of all relevant Feynman diagrams.
It turns out that the way to get rid of the collinear divergence is by renormalizing the \glspl{pdf}.
The idea is very similar to that of renormalized perturbation theory encountered in \cref{sec:renormalization}.
There we started by acknowledging that the fields and couplings appearing in the bare Lagrangian are not physically measurable.
The same goes for the \glspl{pdf} defined in the preceding section.

Consider the \emph{bare} (infinite) \glspl{pdf} $f_i^0(x)$. We can then define the \emph{renormalized} (finite) \glspl{pdf} $f_i(x)$ from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pdf_renormalized}
    f_i^0(x) = f_i(x) + \sum_k \int_x^1 \frac{d\xi}{\xi} f_k\biggl(\frac{x}{\xi}\biggr) \delta_{ik}(\xi),
\end{equation}
where the sum is over the various proton constituents---the partons---and $\delta_{ik}(\xi)$ takes on an analogous role to the counterterms appearing in renormalized perturbation theory in \cref{sec:renormalization}.\footnote{For our purposes it suffices to know that such counterterms can be constructed, and that they eliminate our collinear divergence. For more details on their exact form, see for example Ref.~\cite{Denner:2019vbn}.}
The renormalized $f_i(x)$ are then the \glspl{pdf} extracted from experiments, and the counterterms $\delta_{ik}(\xi)$ contain the infinite part of the bare \glspl{pdf} and can---just as for counterterms in the Lagrangian---be expanded in the renormalized couplings of the theory, starting at \gls{nlo}.
For the \gls{qed} corrections covered in this thesis, this means that $\delta_{ik}(\xi)$ is $\mathcal{O}(\alpha)$.

Similar to how we treated the Lagrangian in \cref{sec:renormalization}, we will consider the \glspl{pdf} appearing in the hadronic cross section in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general} to be the bare \glspl{pdf}.
Using \cref{eq:pdf_renormalized}---and introducing the shorthand $\hat\sigma_{ij}\equiv\hat\sigma(ij\to\slepton^*\slepton+X)$---we can write the hadronic cross section in terms of renormalized \glspl{pdf} instead:
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2} &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i^0(x_1) f_j^0(x_2) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}}{dQ^2}(z)
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, \Biggl\{
            f_i(x_1)f_j(x_2) \Biggl[
                \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}(z)
                + \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO}}}{dQ^2}(z)
            \Biggr]
            \\ &\hspace{9em}
            + \sum_k\int_{x_1}^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi} f_k\biggl(\frac{x_1}{\xi}\biggr) f_j(x_2) \delta_{ik}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}(z)
            \\ &\hspace{9em}
            + \sum_k\int_{x_2}^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi} f_i(x_1) f_k\biggl(\frac{x_2}{\xi}\biggr) \delta_{jk}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}(z)
        \Biggr\} + \mathcal{O}(\alpha^4).
    \label{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized}
    \end{split}
\end{align}
We then want to rewrite this into a form where it is apparent that the \gls{pdf} counterterms can absorb the collinear divergence from the cross section.

Consider first the second line in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized} (the term with the $\delta_{ik}$ counterterm).
By relabeling the indices $(i\leftrightarrow k)$ and shifting $\xi\to x_1/\xi$, we find
\begin{align}
    & \sum_{ijk} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \int_{x_1}^1 \frac{d\xi}{\xi} \, f_k\biggl(\frac{x_1}{\xi}\biggr) f_j(x_2) \delta_{ik}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1x_2}\biggr)
    \nonumber \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \sum_{ijk} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \int_{x_1}^1 \frac{d\xi}{\xi} \, f_i(\xi) f_j(x_2) \delta_{ki}\biggl(\frac{x_1}{\xi}\biggr) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{kj}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1x_2}\biggr)
    \nonumber \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \sum_{ijk} \int_\tau^1 \frac{d\xi}{\xi} \int_{\tau/\xi}^1 dx_2 \int_{\tau/x_2}^\xi dx_1 \, f_i(\xi) f_j(x_2) \delta_{ki}\biggl(\frac{x_1}{\xi}\biggr) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{kj}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1x_2}\biggr),
\end{align}
where we changed the order of integration in the last equality.\footnote{Note, however, that the integrals are not independent, in the sense that $x_1$ appears in the limits of the other two integrals. This is what causes the change in integration bounds in the last line.}
By relabeling the integration variables $(x_1\leftrightarrow\xi)$, and then substituting $\xi\to\xi/x_1$, we end up with
\begin{align}
    & \sum_{ijk} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \int_{x_1}^1 \frac{d\xi}{\xi} \, f_k\biggl(\frac{x_1}{\xi}\biggr) f_j(x_2) \delta_{ik}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1x_2}\biggr)
    \nonumber \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i(x_1) f_j(x_2) \sum_k \int_z^1 d\xi \, \delta_{ki}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{kj}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{z}{\xi}\biggr).
    \label{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized_i}
\end{align}
Following the exact same procedure for the last term in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized}, we find that
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    &\sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, \sum_k\int_{x_2}^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi} f_i(x_1) f_k\biggl(\frac{x_2}{\xi}\biggr) \delta_{jk}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}(z)
    \\ &\hspace{4em}
    = \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 f_i(x_1) f_j(x_2) \sum_k \int_z^1 d\xi \delta_{kj}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ik}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{z}{\xi}\biggr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized_j}
\end{align}

Using \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized_i,eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized_j}, we can write the hadronic cross section in \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_renormalized} as
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\sigma^{\mathrm{NLO}}}{dQ^2} &= \sum_{ij} \int_\tau^1 dx_1 \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 dx_2 \, f_i(x_1) f_j(x_2)
    \\ &\qquad
    \times \Biggl\{
        \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2} + \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO}}}{dQ^2}
        + \sum_k\int_z^1 d\xi \, \Biggl[
            \delta_{ki}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{kj}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{z}{\xi}\biggr)
            + \delta_{kj}(\xi) \frac{d\hat\sigma_{ik}^{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}\biggl(\frac{z}{\xi}\biggr)
        \Biggr]
    \Biggr\}.
\end{split}
\end{align}
We can then eliminate the collinear divergence in the partonic cross section by choosing proper counterterms $\delta_{ij}(\xi)$.

It is worth noting that there is some freedom in how we choose the \gls{pdf} counterterms, just as there was for Lagrangian counterterms.
Consequently, the renormalized \glspl{pdf} also depend on the chosen renormalization scheme.
In this thesis we renormalize \glspl{pdf} in the \gls{msbar} scheme, since this is the one used by \texttt{LHAPDF}~\cite{Buckley:2014ana}---the \texttt{C++} library we will use to extract \glspl{pdf} in \cref{ch:results}.
Note also that the \glspl{pdf} depend on scale, similar to the renormalized fields and couplings in the Lagrangian.
To separate the two scales, we will denote the \emph{factorization scale} associated with \gls{pdf} renormalization by $\mu_F$, and the \emph{renormalization scale} encountered in \cref{sec:shifting_spacetime_dim} by $\mu_R$.



\section{The Hadronic Cross Section}
We are now ready to write down the hadronic cross section for slepton pair production in a form that is fit for numerical evaluation.
First, we should extract the different distributions appearing in the partonic cross section by writing it as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parton_xsec_ws}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma}{dQ^2} = \frac{\sigma_B}{x_1 x_2} \biggl\{
        \wsoft(z)\delta(1-z) + \wrad(z) + \sum_{f} \wf(z) f_+(z)
    \biggr\},
\end{equation}
where the sum over $f$ is over the different plus distributions; $\wsoft$, $\wrad$ and $\wf$ are \emph{coefficient functions}; and $\sigma_B$ is the Born cross section given by
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_B = \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC s Q^5} \fqliab(Q^2).
\end{equation}
Note that $\sigma_B$ is independent of the parton momentum fractions $x_1$ and $x_2$, since it is the hadronic $s$ that appears in the denominator, not the partonic $\shat$.
Furthermore, $\sigma_B$ is---in four spacetime dimensions---related to the partonic prefactors in \cref{eq:summary_sigma0i,eq:summary_sigma0f} by
\begin{align}
    \sigma_B = x_1x_2\hat\sigma_0^i = x_1x_2 \frac{\shat^2 \fqliab(Q^2)}{Q^4 \fqliab(\shat)} \hat\sigma_0^f,
\end{align}
which we will need when reading off the coefficient functions for the \gls{qed} contributions.

We can write down the hadronic cross section by inserting \cref{eq:parton_xsec_ws} into \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_general}, and using the distribution integrals we found in \cref{eq:int_x2_delta,eq:int_x2_plus} to eliminate the delta and plus distributions.
We end up with the following result:\footnote{In this thesis we have only considered quark--anti-quark annihilation, so we ignore the sums over other partons. In addition, it does not matter which proton the (anti-)quark comes from, so it suffices to sum over just the quarks, and compensating with a factor of two.}
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\sigma}{dQ^2}
    &= 2 \sum_q \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} f_q(x_1) f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) x_1x_2 \frac{d\hat\sigma_{q\widebar{q}}}{dQ^2}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= 2 \sigma_B \sum_q \vastl\{
            \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} f_q(x_1) f_{\widebar{q}}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \biggl[
                \wsoft(1) + \sum_f \wf(1) F_f(x_1)
            \biggr]
            \\ &\hspace{5em}
            + \int_\tau^1 \frac{dx_1}{x_1} \int_{\tau/x_1}^1 \frac{dx_2}{x_2} f_q(x_1) \Biggl[
                f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) \wrad\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
                \\ &\hspace{5em}
                + \sum_f \biggl\{
                    f_{\widebar{q}}(x_2) \wf\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
                    - \frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2} f_{\widebar{q}}\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1}\biggr) \wf(1)
                \biggr\} f\biggl(\frac{\tau}{x_1 x_2}\biggr)
            \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
        \label{eq:hadron_xsec_full_w}
    \end{split}
\end{align}
Although this admittedly looks very complicated, it is easier to evaluate numerically than using the general partonic cross section, since there are no distributions left in the integrand.
In addition, part of the two-dimensional integral has been reduced to a one-dimensional integral, which also makes the numerical evaluation more efficient.


\subsection{Hadron-Level Slepton Pair Production}\label{sec:hadronic_cross_section}
We are now ready to write down the coefficient functions for the different contributions to slepton pair production at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}.
% The coefficient functions we give here also carry a subindex denoting which contribution they belong to; \acrlong{lo}---denoted by $\mathrm{LO}$---or corrections to the initial- or final-state---denoted by $i$ and $f$, respectively.

The \gls{lo} contribution to the partonic cross section is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2} = \frac{\sigma_B}{x_1 x_2}\delta(1-z),
\end{equation}
which in the language of \cref{eq:parton_xsec_ws} gives only one coefficient function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadron_w_lo}
    \wsoft_{\mathrm{LO}}(z) = 1.
\end{equation}

The initial-state \gls{nlo} corrections to the partonic cross section were found to be
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^i}{dQ^2} &= \frac{\sigma_B}{x_1 x_2} \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
        \vastl\{
            \Biggl(\frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4\Biggr)\delta(1-z)
            - \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z}
            \\ &\hspace{3em}
            + 2(1+z^2) \plusdistln
            - \frac{1}{\eps}\Biggl[ (1+z^2)\plusdist + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z) \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
Using \cref{eq:summary_G_eps} we can write
\begin{equation}
    G(\eps) \, \frac{1}{\eps} = \biggl[ \frac{1}{\eps} + \ln(4\pi) - \gamma_E \biggr] + \ln\frac{\mu_F^2}{Q^2} + \mathcal{O}(\eps).
\end{equation}
The collinear divergence can be removed by the renormalized \glspl{pdf},\footnote{This is also why we use the factorization scale $\mu_F$ here.} which in the \gls{msbar} scheme leaves only the $\ln(\mu_F^2/Q^2)$ term.
The resulting coefficient functions after renormalizing the \glspl{pdf} are then
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:hadron_w_initial_state}
\begin{align}
    \wsoft_i(z) &= \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} \Biggl\{ \frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4 - \frac{3}{2} \ln\frac{\mu_F^2}{Q^2} \Biggr\},
    \\
    \wrad_i(z) &= -\frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z},
    \\
    w^{\bigl(\frac{1}{1-z}\bigr)_+}_i(z) &= -\frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} (1+z^2) \ln\frac{\mu_F^2}{Q^2},
    \\
    w^{\bigl(\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\bigr)_+}_i(z) &= 2 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} (1+z^2),
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the $i$ subindex denotes that these are coefficient functions for the initial-state corrections.

Lastly, we found the final-state \gls{nlo} corrections to be
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^f}{dQ^2} &= \frac{\sigma_B}{x_1 x_2} \frac{Q^4 \fqliab(\shat)}{\shat^2 \fqliab(Q^2)} \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \vastl\{
            \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda}
            + \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} \plusdist I^r_1
            \\ &\qquad
            + \delta(1-z) \Biggl[
                \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
                - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
                    \ln(4\lambda)
                    + \ln\frac{\mu_R^2}{Q^2}
                \Biggr) I^r_1
                + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
            \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here there are no collinear divergences to worry about, so writing down the coefficient functions is straight-forward:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:hadron_w_final_state}
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        \wsoft_f(z) &= \frac{Q^4 \fqliab(\shat)}{\shat^2 \fqliab(Q^2)} \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \Biggl[
            \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
            \\ &\hspace{9em}
            - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
                \ln(4\lambda)
                + \ln\frac{\mu_R^2}{Q^2}
            \Biggr) I^r_1
            + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
        \Biggr],
    \end{split}
    \\
    \wrad_f(z) &= \frac{Q^4 \fqliab(\shat)}{\shat^2 \fqliab(Q^2)} \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda},
    \\
    w^{\bigl(\frac{1}{1-z}\bigr)_+}_f(z) &= \frac{Q^4 \fqliab(\shat)}{\shat^2 \fqliab(Q^2)} \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} I^r_1,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the subindex $f$ denotes that these are coefficient functions for the final-state corrections.
$g(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2)$, $I_1^r$ and $I_2^r$ were defined in \cref{eq:summary_g,eq:summary_Ir1,eq:summary_Ir2}, and we remind the reader of the K\"allen function shorthands $\lambda \equiv \lambda(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)$ and $\lambda_z \equiv \lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)$.

\Cref{eq:hadron_w_lo,eq:hadron_w_initial_state,eq:hadron_w_final_state} can now be inserted into \cref{eq:hadron_xsec_full_w} and integrated over numerically to obtain the hadronic cross section.
This is the focus of the next chapter.
