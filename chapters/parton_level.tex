\chapter{Parton Level QED Corrections to Slepton Pair Production}\label{ch:parton_level}

In this chapter we shift our focus to the hard scattering process for slepton pair production, where the colliding particles are proton-constituents.
We will calculate the partonic cross section at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}, and use the methods described in \cref{ch:divergences} to handle the various divergences that appear.

We start by discussing the kinematics of our scattering process in \cref{sec:phase_space}, before factoring the cross section in \cref{sec:factorization}. We then calculate the hard scattering cross section at \gls{lo} and \gls{nlo} in \cref{sec:lo,sec:nlo}. Matrix elements are constructed from their associated Feynman diagrams using the Feynman rules given in \cref{tab:slepton_rules}, and all propagators are given in the Feynman gauge.
We will also encounter contractions and traces of Dirac gamma matrices in $d$ dimensions. This is discussed in more detail in \cref{app:dirac_algebra}.


The calculations that follow have been done by hand, with help from the computational software system \texttt{Mathematica}~\cite{Mathematica}, using the following packages: \texttt{FeynArts}~\cite{Hahn:2000kx,Hahn:2001rv} for drawing Feynman diagrams, and extracting their amplitudes; \texttt{FeynCalc}~\cite{Shtabovenko:2016sxi,Shtabovenko:2020gxv,Shtabovenko:2023idz,Mertig:1990an} for simplifying the amplitudes by utilizing four-vectors and Dirac algebra, and rewriting loop-integrals in terms of Passarino--Veltman functions; and \texttt{PackageX}~\cite{Patel:2015tea,Patel:2016fam} for extracting divergent expressions from the Passarino--Veltman functions.
The \texttt{Mathematica} code used in this thesis is available on \texttt{GitHub}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Bragit123/Master_Thesis}}

\section{Phase Space Kinematics}\label{sec:phase_space}
Start by considering a $2 \to n$~scattering process with inital and final momenta $p_i$ and $k_i$, respectively; where particle $k_i$ has mass $m_i$ and energy $E_i$, and the particles $p_i$ are all massless. We further define the \gls{com} energy $\shat = (p_1+p_2)^2$.
The differential cross section for such a process is~\cite{ParticleDataGroup:2026aaa}
\begin{equation}
    d\hat{\sigma} = \frac{1}{2\shat} \abs{\M}^2 d\Pi_{2 \to n},
    \label{eq:dsigma}
\end{equation}
where $d\Pi_{2 \to n}$ is the $n$-body Lorentz invariant differential phase space, given in $d$ dimensions by
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to n} = (2\pi)^d\delta^d \biggl( p_1+p_2 - \sum_{i=1}^n k_i \biggr) \prod_{i=1}^n \frac{d^{d-1}k_i}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_i}.
    \label{eq:dPi_n}
\end{equation}
The hat above $\hat\sigma$ and $\shat$ are there to remind us that they are \textit{partonic} quantities belonging to the hard scattering of proton constituents. It is the hard scattering we will compute perturbatively using Feynman diagrams and \cref{eq:dsigma}. At \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}, the only hard scattering process we consider is quark--anti-quark scattering.
\Cref{eq:dPi_n} is somewhat overly general for our purposes, as we will only consider processes with two or three final-state particles. We will explore the two- and three-body phase spaces in more detail in \cref{sec:two_body_phase_space,sec:three_body_phase_space}, but first we turn to a more general discussion of the kinematic variables that will appear in our calculations.

\subsection{Kinematic Variables}
We will consider the $2\to 2(3)$ partonic scattering in which a quark--anti-quark pair annihilates and produces a pair of sleptons, and possibly emits a photon in the process. This process can be summarized as $q\widebar{q} \to \slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA} (+\gamma)$.
The \gls{lo} diagram for this process is shown in \cref{fig:M_lo}, also displaying the definition of momenta for the quarks and sleptons.
In the case of an emitted photon at \gls{nlo}, we denote its momenta by $k = (\omega, \vec{k})$.
Working with scattering at high energies, we assume the quarks to be massless, and use $m_i$ to denote the mass of slepton $k_i$.
It will be useful to define the sum of quark-momenta $p=p_1+p_2$, with $p^2=\shat$, and the sum of slepton-momenta $q=k_1+k_2$, with the invariant slepton mass $Q^2\equiv q^2$. Note that $Q^2$ is kinematically constrained by
\begin{equation}
    (m_1+m_2)^2 \leq Q^2 \leq \shat.
    \label{eq:Q2_limits}
\end{equation}

To begin with, we consider the \gls{rf} of the incoming quarks.
We are free to choose our coordinate system, and pick the $z$-axis as the line of collision, with the emitted photon constrained to the $xz$-plane.
We let $p_1$ point in the positive $z$-direction, and denote the scattering angle between the incoming quark and emitted photon by $\theta$, as shown in \cref{fig:quark_rf}.
% We are free to choose our coordinate system, and pick the one where the incoming quarks move along the $z$-axis, and the outgoing photon is constrained to the $xz$-plane.
% Letting $p_1$ point in the positive $z$-direction, and the photon is emitted at an angle $\theta$ in the $xz$-plane, as shown in \cref{fig:quark_rf}.
% We are free to choose our coordinate system, and pick the one where $p_1$ points in the positive $z$-direction, and the photon is emitted at an angle $\theta$ in the $xz$-plane, as shown in \cref{fig:quark_rf}.
With this parametrization, we have
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:momenta_quark_rf}
\begin{align}
    p_1^\mu &= \frac{\sqrtshat}{2} (1, 0, 0, 1),
    \label{eq:p1_quark_rf}
    \\
    p_2^\mu &= \frac{\sqrtshat}{2} (1, 0, 0, -1),
    \label{eq:p2_quark_rf}
    \\
    k^\mu &= \frac{\sqrtshat}{2}(1-z) (1, \sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta),
    \label{eq:k_quark_rf}
    \\
    q^\mu &= \frac{\sqrtshat}{2}(1-z) 
    \biggl(\frac{1+z}{1-z}, -\sin\theta, 0, -\cos\theta\biggr),
    \label{eq:q_quark_rf}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $z=\frac{Q^2}{\shat}$ is the amount of initial-state momenta held by the outgoing sleptons, such that the event of no photon radiation is captured by setting $z=1$. The kinematic constraints on $z$ follows directly from \cref{eq:Q2_limits}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{(m_1+m_2)^2}{\shat} \leq z \leq 1.
    \label{eq:z_limits}
\end{equation}

We will also need to know the momenta in the sleptons' \gls{rf}. We can transform from the quarks' to the sleptons' \gls{rf} by performing a rotation around the $y$-axis, followed by a subsequent boost along the $z$-axis.
A rotation of an angle $\theta$ around the $y$-axis is given by the rotation matrix $\Rot_y(\theta)$; and we define the Lorentz boost such that, if a coordinate $x$ in reference frame $S$ transforms to $x^*$ in $S^*$, where $S^*$ moves with a velocity $\vec{\beta} = \beta\ez$ relative to $S$, then we have $x^* = \Boost_z(\beta)x$. On matrix form, the rotation and boost are given by
\begin{align}
    \Rot_y(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix}
        1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & \cos\theta & 0 & \sin\theta \\
        0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
        0 & -\sin\theta & 0 & \cos\theta
    \end{pmatrix}, \quad
    \Boost_z(\beta) = \begin{pmatrix}
        \gamma & 0 & 0 & -\gamma\beta \\
        0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
        -\gamma\beta & 0 & 0 & \gamma
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
where $\gamma = (1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$ is the Lorentz factor.

The sleptons' \gls{rf} is denoted by an asterisk, and is defined as the frame where $q^*=k_1^*+k_2^*=0$.
This condition can be reached by first rotating our coordinates in \cref{fig:quark_rf} by an angle $-\theta$, such that $\vec{q}$ points in the negative $z$-direction; and then perform a boost with velocity $-\beta\ez$, where $\beta = \frac{1-z}{1+z}$. The transformation of a coordinate $x^\mu$ in the quarks' \gls{rf} to $x^{*\mu}$ in the sleptons' \gls{rf} can then be summarized as
\begin{align}
    x^* &= \Boost_z(-\beta) \Rot_y(-\theta) x.
    \label{eq:quark_to_slepton_transform}
\end{align}

From \cref{eq:quark_to_slepton_transform} it is fairly straight-forward to find the quark and photon momenta $p^*$ and $k^*$ in the sleptons' \gls{rf}, and we can parameterize  $k_1^*$ and $k_2^*$ using standard spherical coordinates, as displayed in \cref{fig:slepton_rf}. The four-momenta in the sleptons' \gls{rf} are then
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:momenta_slepton_rf}
\begin{align}
    p^{*\mu} &= \frac{\shat}{2Q}(1-z) \biggl(\frac{1+z}{1-z}, 0, 0, 1\biggr),
    \label{eq:p_slepton_rf}
    \\
    k^{*\mu} &= \frac{\shat}{2Q}(1-z) (1, 0, 0, 1),
    \label{eq:k_slepton_rf}
    \\
    k_i^{*\mu} &= (E_i^*, \pm\kiabs\sin\theta^*\cos\phi^*, \pm\kiabs\sin\theta^*\sin\phi^*, \pm\kiabs\cos\theta^*).
    \label{eq:ki_slepton_rf}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Furthermore, using conservation of energy and momentum, we find
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    E_i^* &= \frac{Q^2 \pm (m_1^2-m_2^2)}{2Q},
    \label{eq:Ei}
    \\
    \kiabs &= \frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q^2, m_1^2, m_2^2)}{2Q},
    \label{eq:kiabs}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\lambda$ is the K\"all\'en function, defined as
\begin{equation}
    \lambda(a,b,c) = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - 2ab - 2ac - 2bc,
    \label{eq:kallen}
\end{equation}
for $a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}$. It is useful to note the bounds of the K\"all\'en function appearing in \cref{eq:kiabs}, which follow from the bounds of $Q^2$ in \cref{eq:Q2_limits}:\footnote{For the upper bound we also set the masses to zero. This imposes a strict inequality, as sleptons are expected to be massive.}
\begin{equation}
    0 \leq \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q^2, m_1^2, m_2^2) < \shat.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two-Body Phase Space}\label{sec:two_body_phase_space}
The two-body phase space is found trivially from \cref{eq:dPi_n}:
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} = (2\pi)^d \delta^d(q-k_1-k_2) \frac{d^{d-1}k_1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1}\frac{d^{d-1}k_2}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2},
    \label{eq:dPi_2}
\end{equation}
where $q=k_1+k_2$, and we choose to work in the \gls{com} frame.\footnote{For the $2\to2$ process considered here, the quarks' and sleptons' \gls{rf}, as well as the \gls{com} frame, are identical. Be careful with variable names, however. For instance: $\theta$ and $\theta^*$ are still different, where $\theta^*$ is the angle of $k_1^*$, and $\theta$ is the angle of $k$ (which does not appear in the $2\to2$ case).}
Using the delta function to get rid of the integration over $\vec{k}_2$, as well as the norm of $\vec{k}_1$, the phase space can be reduced to
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} = \frac{\kiabs^{d-3}}{4(2\pi)^{d-2}Q} d\Omega^*_{d-1},
    \label{eq:dPi_2_dOmega}
\end{equation}
where $d\Omega_d^*$ is the differential solid angle of the $d$-dimensional unit sphere, and $\kiabs$ was given in \cref{eq:kiabs}.
The differential solid angle can be further simplified using $d\Omega_{d-1} = \sin^{d-3}(\theta)d\theta d\Omega_{d-2}$, giving
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} = \frac{\kiabs^{d-3}}{4(2\pi)^{d-2}Q} \sin^{d-3}(\theta^*)d\theta^* d\Omega^*_{d-2},
\end{equation}
with $\theta \in [0, \pi)$.

For any $2\to 2$ process, working in the \gls{com} frame, there is a rotational symmetry around the collision axis. We can therefore integrate out the remaining solid angle. Using the formula for the surface area of a $d$-dimensional unit sphere~\cite{Schwartz:2014sze},
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_d = \frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d}{2}\bigr)},
\end{equation}
the two-body phase space becomes
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} = \frac{\kiabs^{d-3}}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}} \Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr) Q} \sin^{d-3}(\theta^*)d\theta^*.
    \label{eq:dPi_2_k1}
\end{equation}

For future reference, when considering diagrams with photon emission from one of the quarks, we will factor the cross section in such a way that we can consider the emission as a $2\to2$~scattering process with $k$ and $q$ as the final-state momenta. We will then use the quarks' \gls{rf}, where we get a similar expression for the phase space:
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} = \frac{\omega^{d-3}}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}} \Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr) \sqrtshat} \sin^{d-3}(\theta)d\theta.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, we will here introduce the kinematic variable $y=\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos\theta) \in [0,1]$, and substitute this for our angular integration variable $\theta$. Remembering also that $\omega = k^0 = \frac{\sqrtshat}{2}(1-z)$ from \cref{eq:k_quark_rf}, the phase space becomes
\begin{align}
    d\Pi_{2 \to 2} &= \frac{\shat^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(1-z)^{d-3}}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}} \Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}
    \bigl[y(1-y)\bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}dy.
    \label{eq:dPi_2_k}
\end{align}


\subsection{Three-Body Phase Space}\label{sec:three_body_phase_space}
Integrating over the three-body phase space is, perhaps unsurprisingly, significantly more cumbersome than the two-body case, and requires some extra care.
From \cref{eq:dPi_n} it follows that
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2\to 3} = (2\pi)^d \delta^d(p-k_1-k_2-k) 
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_2}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2\omega}.
    \label{eq:dPi_3_general}
\end{equation}
We can rewrite the differential over $\vec{k}$ by changing to spherical coordinates in the \gls{rf} of $\vec{p}$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k}{2\omega} = \frac{\omega^{d-3}}{2} d\omega d\Omega_{d-1}.
    \label{eq:dPi_3_k}
\end{equation}
To simplify the differentials in $\vec{k}_1$ and $\vec{k}_2$, we will switch to the \gls{rf} of $q=k_1+k_2$. Using the properties of the delta function, along with $q^2=Q^2$, we find, similar to \cref{eq:dPi_2_dOmega},
\begin{align}
    \delta^d(p-k_1-k_2-k) 
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_1^*}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1^*}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_2^*}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2^*}
    &= \frac{\kiabs^{d-3}}{4(2\pi)^{d-2}Q} d\Omega^*_{d-1},
    \label{eq:dPi_3_k12}
\end{align}
where $\kiabs$ was defined in \cref{eq:kiabs}.
From \cref{eq:k_quark_rf}, after inserting $z=Q^2/\shat$, we have $\omega = \frac{\shat-Q^2}{2\sqrtshat}$, which combined with \cref{eq:dPi_3_k,eq:dPi_3_k12} gives
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2\to3} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2d-3}} \frac{\omega^{d-3}\kiabs^{d-3}}{16Q\sqrtshat} dQ^2 d\Omega_{d-1} d\Omega_{d-1}^*,
    \label{eq:dPi_3}
\end{equation}
where the integration bounds on $Q^2$ were given in \cref{eq:Q2_limits}.

The $2\to3$~process considered in this thesis appears when a photon radiates from the \gls{lo} diagram in \cref{fig:M_lo}. When the photon is emitted from the initial-state, we will reduce the phase space to that of a $2\to2$~scattering, and use the phase space in \cref{eq:dPi_2_k}.
If the photon is emitted from the final-state, however, we must use the full three-body phase space.
Still, as we will see when factoring the cross section in \cref{sec:factorization}, we can reduce the scattering down to a $1\to3$~decay of an off-shell particle, allowing us to exploit some of the symmetry properties of decays.

More concretely, consider the decay of an initial-state particle with momentum $p$ in its own \gls{rf}, where $\vec{p}=\vec{0}$, and look at the emitted photon of momentum $k$. Since the initial-state particle holds no information about orientation in space,\footnote{At least not after summing over all polarization vectors.} the cross section can not distinguish between different orientations of the photon, and $d\Omega_{d-1}$ can be trivially integrated out.
If we then move to the sleptons' \gls{rf}, as indicated in \cref{eq:dPi_3}, the cross section can be dependent on the direction of $\vec{k}_1$ with respect to $\vec{k}$, captured by the polar angle $\theta^*$; but it can not depend on the azimuthal angle $\phi^*$, because the system is symmetric around the axis of $\vec{k}$.
Setting $d\Omega_{d-1}^* = \sin^{d-3}\theta^* d\theta^* d\Omega_{d-2}^*$, substituting $\theta^*\to\cos\theta^*$, and integrating over the remaining solid angles $d\Omega_{d-1}$ and $d\Omega_{d-2}^*$, \cref{eq:dPi_3} then becomes
\begin{align}
    d\Pi_{1\to3} &=
    \frac{1}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-1}{2}\bigr)}
    \frac{1}{(4\pi)^{(2d-3)/2}} \frac{\omega^{d-3}\kiabs^{d-3}}{4Q\sqrtshat} dQ^2 (1-\cos^2\theta^*)^{\frac{d-4}{2}} d(\cos\theta^*).
\end{align}
The two Gamma functions appearing in the denominator can be combined using the Legendre duplication formula:
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma(t)\Gamma\Bigl( t + \frac{1}{2} \Bigr) = 2^{1-2t} \sqrt{\pi} \Gamma(2t),
    \label{eq:legendre_duplication}
\end{equation}
resulting in
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{1\to3} =
    \frac{\omega^{d-3}\kiabs^{d-3}}{8(2\pi)^{d-1} \Gamma\bigl( d-2 \bigr) Q\sqrtshat} (1-\cos^2\theta^*)^{\frac{d-4}{2}} d(\cos\theta^*) dQ^2.
    \label{eq:dPi_13_before_x}
\end{equation}

Lastly, we also make a change of variables that might seem curious at this point, but will turn out to be extremely helpful, namely
\begin{equation}
    x \equiv \lambda^\frac{1}{2}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2) \cos\theta^* + \mt_2^2 - \mt_1^2,
    \label{eq:x_def}
\end{equation}
where we have defined the dimensionless slepton masses $\mt_i \equiv m_i/Q$. We substitute $\cos\theta^* \to x$, where the integration bounds for $x$ are
\begin{equation}
    \xpm = \mt_2^2 - \mt_1^2 \pm \lambda^\frac{1}{2}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2).
    \label{eq:xpm}
\end{equation}
Using that
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)
    &= \sqrt{(1+\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2)^2-4\mt_1^2}
    < 1+\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2,
    \\
    \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)
    &= \sqrt{(1-\mt_1^2+\mt_2^2)^2-4\mt_2^2}
    < 1-\mt_1^2+\mt_2^2,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
we see that $\xpm$ are further constrained by
\begin{equation}
    -1 < \xm \leq x \leq \xp < 1.
    \label{eq:xpm_lim}
\end{equation}
We can now rewrite \cref{eq:dPi_13_before_x} as
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{1\to3} =
    \biggl(\frac{Q^2}{4\pi}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \biggl(\frac{\shat}{4\pi}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \frac{(1-z)^{d-3}}{16\pi^3 \cdot 2^d \Gamma(d-2)}
    \Bigl[ (\xp-x)(x-\xm) \Bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    dx dQ^2.
    \label{eq:dPi_13}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Effective Couplings for Initial- and Final-State Diagrams}
The process considered in this thesis is the inclusive production of two sleptons, $q\widebar{q}\to\slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA}(+X)$.
At \gls{lo} there are two diagrams contributing, namely the s-channel diagrams with an intermediate photon or $Z$~boson, as shown in \cref{fig:M_lo}.\footnote{Note that we give propagators in the Feynman gauge, and should therefore, in principle, also include the neutral Goldstone boson $G^0$ in order to maintain gauge invariance. Nevertheless, for the \gls{qed} corrections considered in this thesis, all diagrams involving $G^0$ include a $q\widebar{q}G^0$ interaction vertex, which is proportional to the quark Yukawa couplings~\cite{Rosiek:1989sce}. Consequently, these diagrams belong to a set of corrections which we do not consider---we assume the quark masses to be negligible---and will be ignored.}

% Hence any diagram involving $G^0$ will be proportional to the $q\widebar{q}G^0$ coupling, which is proportional to the Yukawa coupling~\cite{Rosiek:1989sce}, which in turn is proportional to the quark masses, which we consider to be negligibly small.
% Therefore, although the Goldstone boson should in principle be accounted for, it belongs to a set corrections which we do not consider.

We can simplify the calculation of \gls{nlo} corrections by factoring the \gls{lo} cross section, splitting the diagram in \cref{fig:M_lo} into two subprocesses: an initial-state scattering $q\widebar{q}\to\gamma/Z$, followed by a subsequent decay $\gamma/Z\to\slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA}$.
We then consider \gls{nlo} corrections in two distinct ways: one where we allow corrections to the initial-state scattering while keeping the final-state fixed, and one where we allow corrections to the final-state decay while keeping the initial-state fixed.
In \cref{fig:M_factored} we present the Feynman diagrams for these corrections, 
where the blobs are to be understood as effective couplings, encompassing all \gls{qed} corrections at \gls{nlo} that come from adding photon-lines to the \gls{lo} diagrams.
The light gray external photon lines represent the possible emission of an extra photon of momentum $k$, as needed when considering the \textit{inclusive} cross section at \gls{nlo}.
To ease the notation going forward, we will consider such emitted photons as being part of the blobs in \cref{fig:M_factored}, allowing the blobs to represent corrections for both the $2\to2$ and $2\to3$~scattering processes.

As mentioned, the blobs represent \gls{nlo} corrections that add photon lines to the \gls{lo} diagram in \cref{fig:M_lo}. 
Therefore, even at \gls{nlo}, our diagrams will still contain the $q\widebar{q}(\gamma/Z)$ vertex, and must either contain the $\slepton\slepton^*(\gamma/Z)$ or $\slepton\slepton^*\gamma(\gamma/Z)$ vertex.
We can therefore factor out part of the "blob" coupling, which will be common to all considered diagrams. Applying the Feynman rules in \cref{tab:slepton_rules} gives the following "effective" Feynman rules:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:factor}
\begin{equation}
    \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{rules/factor_left}
    = \begin{cases}
        -ieQ_q \Gamma^\mu \,,\quad (\gamma)
        \\
        \frac{ie}{\sw\cw} \Gamma^\mu  \Gamma^5 \,,\quad (Z)
    \end{cases} \label{eq:factor_left}
\end{equation}
for initial-state corrections, and
\begin{equation}
    \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{rules/factor_right}
    = \begin{cases}
        ie \delta^{AB} K^\mu \,,\quad (\gamma)
        \\
        \frac{ie}{\sw\cw} \zliab K^\mu \,,\quad (Z)
    \end{cases}, \label{eq:factor_right}
\end{equation}
% \begin{align}
%     \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{rules/factor_left}
%     &= \begin{cases}
%         -ieQ_q \Gamma^\mu \,,\quad (\gamma)
%         \\
%         \frac{ie}{\sw\cw} \Gamma^\mu  \Gamma^5 \,,\quad (Z)
%     \end{cases} \label{eq:factor_left}
%     \\
%     \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{rules/factor_right}
%     &= \begin{cases}
%         ie \delta^{AB} K^\mu \,,\quad (\gamma)
%         \\
%         \frac{ie}{\sw\cw} \zliab K^\mu \,,\quad (Z)
%     \end{cases}, \label{eq:factor_right}
% \end{align}
\end{subequations}
for final-state corrections;
where we have defined the shorthand $\Gamma^5 = \zql(1-\gamma^5)+\zqr(1+\gamma^5)$. $\Gamma^\mu$ and $K^\mu$ represent tensor structures containing the remaining part of the diagrams, such that at \gls{lo} we have $\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu$ and $K^\mu=(k_2-k_1)^\mu$.
Note in particular that $\Gamma^\mu$ has Dirac structure, as it will contain gamma matrices,\footnote{It will, however, not contain $\gamma^5$, as we only consider corrections in \gls{qed}, which is chirally symmetric.} while $K^\mu$ does not. In addition, from the available momenta in the diagrams we can see that $\Gamma^\mu$ depends on $k_1$ and $k_2$ only through $q$, and similarly $K^\mu$ depends on $p_1$ and $p_2$ only through $p$.
One should also note that the chiral factor $\Gamma^5$ in the $q\widebar{q}Z$ coupling of \cref{eq:factor_left} has been moved to the right. With each additional photon propagating from one of the quark lines, we get an extra $q\widebar{q}\gamma$ coupling, as well as a quark propagator. We therefore always add an even number of gamma matrices, allowing $\Gamma^5$ to safely move past them.
Since the blobs represent corrections both with and without photon emission, $\Gamma^\mu$ and $K^\mu$ may also contain photon polarization vectors.

Before moving further, let us briefly discuss which assumptions we have made, and how they limit the generalization of our procedure.
The perhaps most obvious limitation is that our factorization only works when considering higher-order corrections to either the initial- or final-state of the process.
For diagrams involving a photon-line between the initial- and final-state, or interferences between initial- and final-state emission, we will have to turn back to consider their full diagrams without factorization. We will consider such non-factorizable diagrams in \cref{sec:non_factorizable}.
The other assumption we made, which is important for the way we wrote down the effective couplings in \cref{eq:factor_left,eq:factor_right}, is that the \gls{nlo} corrections add photon lines to the \gls{lo} diagram. This does not hold for supersymmetric corrections, such as the one in \cref{fig:M_susy}. This limitation is good to keep in mind, although we will not consider supersymmetric corrections in this thesis, as their contributions are expected to be tiny compared to Standard Model corrections.

    \label{fig:M_susy}

\subsection{The Squared Amplitude}
Let us first consider the case where we leave the final-state as it was at \gls{lo}, while allowing corrections to the initial-state. This process is encapsulated in \cref{fig:factor_left}, and using our newfound Feynman rules for the effective coupling in \cref{eq:factor_left}, we can write down the amplitude. 
To declutter notation we introduce the shorthands $u_1 \equiv u(p_1)$ and $\widebar{v}_2 \equiv \widebar{v}(p_2)$ for the Dirac spinors, and---anticipating the use of dimensional regularization at \gls{nlo}---we work in $d=4-2\eps$ dimensions:
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \M^i_\gamma &= -\frac{e^2Q_q\mu^{4-d}\delta^{AB}}{Q^2} \widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu u_1 (k_2-k_1)_\mu,
    \\
    \M^i_Z &= \frac{e^2\mu^{4-d}\zliab}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{1}{Q^2-m_Z^2+im_Z\Gamma_Z}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1 (k_2-k_1)_\mu,
\end{align}
which put together gives
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \M^i = \M^i_\gamma+\M^i_Z
        &= -e^2\mu^{4-d}(k_2-k_1)_\mu 
        \\ &\qquad \times
        \biggl\{
            \frac{Q_q\delta^{AB}}{Q^2}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu u_1 - \frac{\zliab}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{1}{Q^2-m_Z^2+im_Z\Gamma_Z}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1
        \biggr\},
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
% \begin{subequations}
% \begin{align}
%     \M^i_\gamma &= -\frac{e^2Q_q\mu^{4-d}\delta^{AB}}{Q^2} \widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu u_1 (k_2-k_1)_\mu,
%     \\
%     \M^i_Z &= \frac{e^2\mu^{4-d}\zliab}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{1}{Q^2-m_Z^2+im_Z\Gamma_Z}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1 (k_2-k_1)_\mu,
%     \\
%     \begin{split}
%         \M^i = \M^i_\gamma+\M^i_Z
%         &= -e^2\mu^{4-d}(k_2-k_1)_\mu 
%         \\ &\qquad \times
%         \biggl\{
%             \frac{Q_q\delta^{AB}}{Q^2}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu u_1 - \frac{\zliab}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{1}{Q^2-m_Z^2+im_Z\Gamma_Z}\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1
%         \biggr\},
%     \end{split}
% \end{align}
% \end{subequations}
where $\mu$ is the renormalization scale introduced in \cref{sec:shifting_spacetime_dim}, and $\NC=3$ is the number of quark colors.
The upper index $i$ is there to remind us that this considers initial-state corrections.
Using the completeness relations for spinors, given by~\cite{Peskin:1995ev}
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{\text{spin}} u^s(p)\widebar{u}^s(p) = \pslash + m, \quad
    \sum_{\text{spin}} v^s(p)\widebar{v}^s(p) = \pslash - m,
\end{equation}
we can compute the spin- and color-averaged squared amplitude:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \overline{|\M^i|^2}
    &= \frac{e^4\mu^{2(4-d)}}{4\NC} (k_2-k_1)_\mu (k_2-k_1)_\nu \Biggl\{
        \frac{Q_q^2\delta^{AB}}{Q^4}\Tr\Bigl[\ptwoslash\Gamma^\mu \poneslash\widebar\Gamma^\nu \Bigr]
        \\ &\qquad
        - 2\Re\biggl\{ \frac{Q_q\delta^{AB}\zliab}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{1}{Q^2\bigl[ Q^2-m_Z^2+im_Z\Gamma_Z \bigr]} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\Gamma^\mu\poneslash\widebar\Gamma^\nu\Gamma^5 \Bigr] \biggr\}
        \\ &\qquad
        + \frac{(\zliab)^2}{\sw^4\cw^4}\frac{1}{(Q^2-m_Z^2)^2+m_Z^2\Gamma_Z^2}\Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\Gamma^\mu\poneslash\widebar\Gamma^\nu \Gamma^5\Gamma^5 \Bigr]
    \Biggr\}, \label{eq:Mi_squared_pre_trace}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where we understand $\widebar\Gamma^\mu$ to be $\Gamma^\mu$ with all gamma matrices in reverse order and c-numbers complex conjugated, so as to fulfill $(\widebar{v}_2\Gamma^\mu u_1)^\dagger = \widebar{u}_1 \widebar\Gamma^\mu v_2$. Note also that, in the $2\to3$ case where an extra photon is emitted, there is an implicit sum over photon polarization vectors.

As discussed in \cref{app:dirac_algebra}, the treatment of $\gamma^5$ is ambiguous in $d$ dimensions, and the prescription we choose to work with in this thesis is the \gls{ndr} scheme. The calculations up until this point are still valid in this scheme, but the traces involving a single $\gamma^5$ must be treated with a bit more care. We will consider these traces in four spacetime dimensions, and show that their contributions to the squared amplitude is zero, and therefore need not be regulated, but can simply be discarded.

As shown in \cref{app:gamma5}, the trace of $\gamma^5$ with an odd number of $\gamma^\mu$ is zero, while the trace of $\gamma^5$ with an even number of $\gamma^\mu$ reduces to a sum of terms proportional to the Levi-Civita tensor together with metric tensors, for different permutations of the involved indices:\footnote{See \cref{app:gamma5}.}
\begin{equation}
    \Tr[\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n}}\gamma^5] = 
    \sum_{\mathclap{ \substack{\text{permutations} \\ \text{of }\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{2n}}}} 
    \Bigl(\propto\eps^{\mu_1\mu_2\mu_3\mu_4} g^{\mu_5\mu_6}\cdots g^{\mu_{2n-1}\mu_{2n}}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Since the squared amplitude is a scalar quantity, those Lorentz indices must be contracted with Lorentz indices from the remaining expression in \cref{eq:Mi_squared_pre_trace}. The only available tensors are the four-momenta\footnote{Remember that $\Gamma^\mu$ only depends on $k_1$ and $k_2$ through $q$.} $p_1^\mu$, $p_2^\mu$, $k^\mu$ and $q^\mu$---one of which can be eliminated by four-momentum conseration---and the metric tensor $g^{\mu\nu}$.

Consider the term with $\eps^{\mu_1\mu_2\mu_3\mu_4}$. Contracting this with anything that is symmetric in two of those indices yield zero because of the anti-symmetry of the Levi-Civita tensor, so the only possible non-zero case is if the tensor it is contracted with contains all the available tensors: $p_{1\mu_1}p_{2\mu_2}q_{\mu_3}g_{\mu_4\mu_5}$. However, since there is an even number of indices, and there are only metric tensors left to contract with our expression, $g_{\mu_4\mu_5}$ must at some point get contracted with $p_1^{\mu_5}$, $p_2^{\mu_5}$ or $q^{\mu_5}$, resulting in a tensor that is symmetric in at least two of the indices $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_4$. Therefore, all traces with a single $\gamma^5$ becomes zero when contracted with the remaining part of the cross section.
Since these contributions are zero in four spacetime dimensions, they can be discarded also in $d$ dimensions.

For the remaining traces with an even number of $\gamma^5$ we return to $d$ dimensions.
Using the fact that $\gamma^5$ squares to unity, we can show that the three terms in \cref{eq:Mi_squared_pre_trace} are actually proportional, and we can rewrite the squared amplitude as
\begin{align}
    \overline{|\M^i|^2} = \frac{16\pi^2\alpha^2\mu^{2(4-d)}}{Q^4}\fqliab(Q^2) \frac{1}{4\NC} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\Gamma^\mu\poneslash\widebar\Gamma^\nu \Bigr] (k_2-k_1)_\mu (k_2-k_1)_\nu, \label{eq:Mi_squared}
\end{align}
where we have defined the effective coupling
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \fqliab(Q^2) &= Q_q^2\delta^{AB}
    - \frac{2Q_q\delta^{AB}\zliab(\zql+\zqr)}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{Q^2(Q^2-m_Z^2)}{(Q^2-m_Z^2)^2+m_Z^2\Gamma_Z^2}
    \\ &\qquad
    + \frac{2\bigl(\zliab\bigr)^2(\zql^2+\zqr^2)}{\sw^4\cw^4}\frac{Q^4}{(Q^2-m_Z^2)^2+m_Z^2\Gamma_Z^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

The result for final-state corrections is derived in the same way, starting from \cref{fig:factor_right}. The squared amplitude is then similar to \cref{eq:Mi_squared}, but with the following differences:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The intermediate vector boson has momentum $p$, instead of $q$, with $p^2=\shat$.
    \item The initial-state coupling reduces to the \gls{lo} case, so $\Gamma^\mu$ becomes $\gamma^\mu$.
    \item We use the effective coupling on the final-state as given in \cref{eq:factor_right}, so we must substitute $(k_2-k_1)^\mu \to K^\mu$, where we remind the reader that $K^\mu$, as opposed to $\Gamma^\mu$, does depend on $k_1$ and $k_2$, but it depends on $p_1$ and $p_2$ only through $p$.
\end{itemize}
The resulting spin- and color-averaged squared amplitude for final-state corrections is then
\begin{equation}
    \overline{|\M^f|^2} = \frac{16\pi^2\alpha^2\mu^{2(4-d)}}{\shat^2}\fqliab(\shat) \frac{1}{4\NC} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu\poneslash\gamma^\nu \Bigr] K_\mu K_\nu. \label{eq:Mf_squared}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Differential Cross Section}
The differential cross section is given by \cref{eq:dsigma}
\begin{equation}
    d\hat{\sigma} = \frac{1}{2\shat} \overline{|\M|^2} d\Pi.
\end{equation}
For final-state corrections, as seen in \cref{eq:Mf_squared}, all dependence on phase space momenta are contained in $K_\mu K_\nu$, and we can write the final-state differential cross section as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f}{dQ^2} = \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\shat^3} \fqliab(\shat) 
    X_f^{\mu\nu}
    L_{\mu\nu},
    \label{eq:dsigmaf_LX}
\end{equation}
where we have defined the tensors
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    L_{\mu\nu} &= \mu^{4-d} \int d\Pi_L K_\mu K_\nu,
    \\
    X_f^{\mu\nu} &= \frac{\mu^{4-d}}{4\NC} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu\poneslash\gamma^\nu \Bigr],
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $d\Pi_L$ defined by extracting the differential $dQ^2$ from \cref{eq:dPi_3}, such that
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi = dQ^2 d\Pi_L.
    \label{eq:dPi_L_def}
\end{equation}

We can compute $X_f^{\mu\nu}$ using the trace relations for gamma matrices given in \cref{app:gamma_traces}, and since it does not acquire any higher-order corrections, we can safely set $d=4$,\footnote{
    One can in principle imagine terms proportional to $\eps$ giving finite contributions when multiplied by $1/\eps$ terms from $L_{\mu\nu}$.
    However, since $X_f^{\mu\nu}$ does not obtain higher order corrections, its result is the same for all \gls{nlo} diagrams.
    If $X_f^{\mu\nu}$ contributes $\alpha_0 + \alpha_1\eps$, and $L_{\mu\nu}$ contributes $\beta_0 + \beta_1/\eps$, their infinite contribution would be $\alpha_0\beta_1/\eps$. If we have included all the necessary contributions to the inclusive cross section, and have renormalized the result properly, the infinities has to disappear, meaning $\beta_1=0$. Thus the possibly finite term $\alpha_1\beta_1$ is also zero. It is therefore safe to set $d=4$ for $X_f^{\mu\nu}$ from the start.
    \label{foot:d=4_argument}
} giving
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f}{dQ^2} = \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\NC\shat^3}\fqliab(\shat) \biggl(p_{1\mu} p_{2\nu} + p_{1\nu} p_{2\mu} - \frac{\shat}{2}g_{\mu\nu} \biggr) L^{\mu\nu}.
    \label{eq:dsigmaf_tensor}
\end{equation}

We now turn to initial-state corrections. The squared amplitude in \cref{eq:Mi_squared} has a Dirac trace that only depends on $k_1$ and $k_2$ implicitly through $q$, thus we can simplify the differential cross section further in this case by also factoring the phase space.
We consider the phase space for a $2\to3$~scattering process in \cref{eq:dPi_3_general}, restated here for convenience:\footnote{In the case of no photon emission, $k$ can be trivially integrated out.}
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_{2\to 3} = (2\pi)^d \delta^d(p-k_1-k_2-k) 
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k_2}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2}
    \frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2\omega}.
\end{equation}
We multiply by the factors $\frac{d^d q}{(2\pi)^d} (2\pi)^d \delta^d(k+q-p_1-p_2)$ and $\delta(q^2-Q^2)dQ^2$, which both integrate to unity. The phase space then becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    d\Pi &= (2\pi)^d \delta^d(q+k-p_1-p_2) \frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2\omega} \delta(q^2-Q^2)dQ^2
    \\ &\qquad \times
    (2\pi)^d \delta^d(k_1+k_2-q) \frac{d^{d-1}k_1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1}\frac{d^{d-1}k_2}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

We can use $\delta(q^2-Q^2)$ to get rid of the integral over the zeroth component of $d^dq$, leaving us with the factored phase space
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi = \frac{1}{2\pi} d\Pi_X d\Pi_H dQ^2,
    \label{eq:phase_space_factored}
\end{equation}
where we have defined the hadronic and sleptonic phase spaces:
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    d\Pi_H &= (2\pi)^d \delta^d(q+k-p_1-p_2) \frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2q^0}\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2\omega},
    \label{eq:phase_space_H}
    \\
    d\Pi_X &= (2\pi)^d \delta^d(k_1+k_2-q) \frac{d^{d-1}k_1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_1}\frac{d^{d-1}k_2}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2E_2}.
    \label{eq:phase_space_L}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Using this, we can write the differential cross section for initial-state corrections as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i}{dQ^2} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{\shat Q^4}\fqliab(Q^2)
    H^{\mu\nu} X^i_{\mu\nu},
    \label{eq:dsigmai_HX}
\end{equation}
where we have defined the following tensors:
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    H^{\mu\nu} &= \frac{\mu^{4-d}}{4\NC}\int d\Pi_H \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\Gamma^\mu\poneslash\widebar\Gamma^\nu \Bigr],
    \\
    X_i^{\mu\nu} &= \mu^{4-d} \int d\Pi_X (k_2-k_1)^\mu(k_2-k_1)^\nu.
    \label{eq:Ximunu}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

As with $X_f^{\mu\nu}$ in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_LX}, $X_i^{\mu\nu}$ does not receive any higher-order corrections, so we can evaluate it in $d=4$ dimensions.\footnote{See \cref{foot:d=4_argument}.}
With the available tensors, it is clear that it must have the form\footnote{The factor $Q^2$ is inserted so that $X_1(Q)$ and $X_2(Q)$ are both dimensionless quantities.}
\begin{equation}
    X_i^{\mu\nu} = X_1(Q) Q^2 g^{\mu\nu} + X_2(Q) q^\mu q^\nu.
    \label{eq:X_decomp}
\end{equation}
Contracting with $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $q_\mu q_\nu$ we get the following coupled equations
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    g_{\mu\nu}X_i^{\mu\nu} &= Q^2 \bigl[ 4 X_1(Q) + X_2(Q) \bigr],
    \\
    q_\mu q_\nu X_i^{\mu\nu} &= Q^4 \bigl[ X_1(Q) + X_2(Q) \bigr],
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
which can be solved to give the following expression for $X_1(Q)$:
\begin{align}
    X_1(Q) &= -\frac{1}{3Q^4} \Bigl[ q_\mu q_\nu X_i^{\mu\nu} - Q^2 g_{\mu\nu} X_i^{\mu\nu} \Bigr].
    \label{eq:X1_decomp}
\end{align}

We could in principle also calculate $X_2(Q)$, but it is not necessary as the second term in \cref{eq:X_decomp} disappears when contracting with $H^{\mu\nu}$ by the Ward identity.
Inserting \cref{eq:Ximunu} into \cref{eq:X1_decomp}, and using that $q=k_1+k_2$, we can show that (with $d=4$):
\begin{equation}
    X_1(Q) = -\frac{4\kiabs^2}{3Q^2} \int d\Pi_X
    = -\frac{\kiabs^3}{3\pi Q^3},
    \label{eq:X1}
\end{equation}
where the integral over $d\Pi_X$ follows from \cref{eq:dPi_2_k1} with $d=4$, and $\kiabs$ was defined in \cref{eq:kiabs}. We can now insert \cref{eq:X_decomp} into \cref{eq:dsigmai_HX} with our expression in \cref{eq:X1} for $X_1(Q)$, resulting in
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i}{dQ^2} = -\frac{4\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2)
    g_{\mu\nu} H^{\mu\nu}.
    \label{eq:dsigmai_tensor}
\end{equation}

The remaining tensors $H^{\mu\nu}$ and $L^{\mu\nu}$ in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor,eq:dsigmai_tensor} can be rewritten in terms of Feynman diagrams involving an "effective" photon $\gamma^*$. Its Feynman rules are the same as that of the photon, but with some numerical factors left out:\footnote{The same numerical factors that appear in front of $\Gamma^\mu$ and $K^\mu$ in \cref{eq:factor}.}
\begin{equation}
    \inputtikz[baseline=(v)]{rules/peff_q_qbar} = \gamma^\mu,
    \quad
    \inputtikz[baseline=(v)]{rules/peff_l_l} = (k_2-k_1)^\mu,
    \quad
    \inputtikz[baseline=(v)]{rules/peff_p_l_l} = 2eg^{\mu\nu}.
    \label{eq:eff_photon_rules}
\end{equation}
From these Feynman rules we have:\footnote{Note that we average over the incoming quark--anti-quark pair in $H^{\mu\nu}$, but not in $L^{\mu\nu}$.}
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \eps_\mu(q) \eps_\nu^*(q) H^{\mu\nu} &\equiv \mu^{4-d} \int d\Pi_H \overline{|\M(q\widebar{q} \to \gamma^*(+\gamma)|^2},
    \label{eq:H_munu}
    \\
    \eps_\mu(p) \eps_\nu^*(p) L^{\mu\nu} &\equiv \mu^{4-d} \int d\Pi_L |\M(\gamma^* \to \slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA} (+\gamma)|^2.
    \label{eq:L_munu}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Using that $-g_{\mu\nu}$ is equal to the sum of polarizations $\eps_\mu\eps^*_\nu$, we can simplify the initial-state cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmai_tensor} even further, giving
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i}{dQ^2} &= \frac{4\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2) \eps_\mu(q)\eps^*_\nu(q) H^{\mu\nu}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{4\alpha^2 \mu^{4-d} \kiabs^3}{3\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2) 
    \int d\Pi_H \overline{\abs{\M(q\widebar{q}\to\gamma^*(+\gamma)}^2}.
    \label{eq:dsigmai_scalar}
\end{align}

To summarize this section: we have laid the groundwork for doing calculations at \gls{nlo}, by simplifying in two major ways:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We have reduced what was originally a $2\to2(3)$~scattering process down to a $2\to1(2)$~scattering for initial-state corrections, given in \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar}; and $1\to2(3)$ decay for final-state corrections, given in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor}; greatly simplifying the phase space integration.
    \item To begin with, we had to consider two versions of all diagrams; one with an intermediate photon, and one with a $Z$~boson. This has now been reduced to a single effective photon $\gamma^*$ which incorporates both vector bosons through the coupling $\fqliab(Q^2)$.
\end{itemize}


\section{The Leading Order Cross Section}\label{sec:lo}
We will calculate the cross section at \gls{lo} in two ways, using the initial-state and final-state expressions in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor,eq:dsigmai_tensor}.
Both methods will produce the same result,\footnote{At least in four spacetime dimensions. Since we set $d=4$ in part of \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor,eq:dsigmai_scalar}, the result will be slightly different in the two cases when working in $d=4-2\eps$ dimensions.}
since $\shat$ and $Q^2$ are equal---there is no photon emission at \gls{lo}---but keeping track of how $\shat$ and $Q^2$ originate in the two expressions will be helpful when writing \gls{nlo} calculations in terms of the \gls{lo} result.

\end{figure}

\subsection{The Initial-State Way}
The initial-state \gls{lo} Feynman diagram is shown in \cref{fig:M_lo_left}, giving the amplitude
\begin{align}\label{eq:Mi_lo}
    i\M^i_0 &= \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\mu u_1 \eps^*_\mu(q).
\end{align}
The spin- and color-averaged squared amplitude is then
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\abs{\M^i_0}^2} = \frac{-g_{\mu\nu}}{4\NC}\Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash \gamma^\mu \poneslash \gamma^\nu \Bigr]
    = \frac{d-2}{2\NC}\shat,
\end{equation}
where we have used \cref{eq:gamma_contraction_1}.
Inserting this into \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar}, we find
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i_\mathrm{LO}}{dQ^2} &= \frac{4\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2) 
    \mu^{4-d} \frac{d-2}{2\NC}\shat \int \frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{1}{2q^0} (2\pi)^d \delta^d(q-p_1-p_2)
    \nonumber \\
    &= \biggl\{ \frac{d-2}{2} \mu^{4-d} \biggr\}
    \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2) \delta(1-z)
    \nonumber \\
    &\equiv \hat{\sigma}^i_{0} \delta(1-z),
    \label{eq:sigma0i}
\end{align}
where the factor in curly brackets reduces to unity in $d=4$ spacetime dimensions.

\subsection{The Final-State Way}
The final-state \gls{lo} Feynman diagram is shown in \cref{fig:M_lo_right}, and we can write down its amplitude:
\begin{align}
    i\M^f_0 &= (k_2-k_1)^\mu\eps_\mu(p).
    \label{eq:Mf0}
\end{align}
Inserting into \cref{eq:L_munu} we have
\begin{equation}
    L^{\mu\nu} = \mu^{4-d} \int d\Pi_L (k_2-k_1)^\mu (k_2-k_1)^\nu.
    \label{eq:L_munu_lo}
\end{equation}
Using the momenta given in \cref{eq:p1_quark_rf,eq:p2_quark_rf,eq:ki_slepton_rf}\footnote{We are allowed to combine these, since the quarks' and sleptons' \glspl{rf} are the same at \gls{lo}.} we can find the cross section from \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor}:
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f_\mathrm{LO}}{dQ^2} &= \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\NC\shat^3}\fqliab(\shat) \biggl(p_{1\mu} p_{2\nu} + p_{1\nu} p_{2\mu} - \frac{\shat}{2}g_{\mu\nu} \biggr) L^{\mu\nu}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\NC\shat^3}\fqliab(\shat) \mu^{4-d}
    2 \kiabs^2 \delta(1-z) \int d\Pi \sin^2\theta^*.
    \label{eq:sigmaf_lo_dpi}
\end{align}
The last integral is found using \cref{eq:dPi_2_k1}:
\begin{align}
    \int d\Pi \sin^2\theta^* &= \frac{\kiabs^{d-3}}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{d-2}{2})Q}
    \int d\theta^* \sin^{d-1}{\theta^*}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{\kiabs}{6\pi Q}
    \frac{3(d-2)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}{2(d-1)\Gamma(d-2)} \biggl(\frac{\kiabs^2}{\pi}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}},
\end{align}
which inserted back into \cref{eq:sigmaf_lo_dpi} gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f_\mathrm{LO}}{dQ^2} &= \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\NC\shat^3}\fqliab(\shat)
    2 \kiabs^2
    \frac{\kiabs}{6\pi Q}
    \frac{3(d-2)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}{2(d-1)\Gamma(d-2)}
    \biggl(\frac{\pi \mu^2}{\kiabs^2}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}} \delta(1-z)
    \nonumber \\
    &= \Biggl\{
        \frac{3(d-2)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}{2(d-1)\Gamma(d-2)}
        \biggl(\frac{4\pi \mu^2}{Q^2}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}} \lambda^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)
    \Biggr\}
    \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\shat^3 Q}\fqliab(\shat)
    \delta(1-z)
    \nonumber \\
    &\equiv \hat{\sigma}^f_0 \delta(1-z),
    \label{eq:sigma0f}
\end{align}
where we have used the dimensionless slepton masses $\mt_i=m_i/Q$, and the factor inside the curly brackets reduces to unity in $d=4$ spacetime dimensions.
Note also that if we set $Q^2 = \shat$ and $d=4$, \cref{eq:sigma0i,eq:sigma0f} give the same result, which is expected, as it should not matter \textit{how} we compute the \gls{lo} cross section.

\section{Next-To-Leading Order Corrections}\label{sec:nlo}
In this section we will compute corrections to slepton pair production at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}.
Due to the amount of contributing diagrams, we have divided the section into three subparts, based on the type of corrections that appear: initial-state, final-state, and non-factorizable corrections.
Each part is then further subdivided into corrections that contain virtual loops, and those involving real photon emission. Note that we will denote both loop momenta and the momentum of an emitted photon by $k$. This should not cause any confusion, as there are no diagrams at \gls{nlo} that contain both.

\subsection{Initial-State Corrections}\label{sec:initial_state}

As a reminder, for initial-state corrections we use the differential cross section defined in \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar}. Consequently, all diagrams considered for initial-state corrections are understood to signify the process $q\widebar{q} \to \gamma^*(+\gamma)$, for which matrix elements correspond to the matrix element in \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar}.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \inputtikz{feynman_diagrams/initial_state/is_virt}
    \caption{Feynman diagram for virtual \acrshort{qed} correction to the initial-state.}
    \label{fig:is_virt}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Virtual Contribution}
There is only one initial-state diagram involving a virtual photon, shown in \cref{fig:is_virt}. Its matrix element is given by
\begin{equation}
    i\M^i_v = -ie^2Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d} \eps^*_\mu(q) \widebar{v}_2
    \Biggl(
        \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{\gamma^\nu (\kslash-\ptwoslash)\gamma^\mu(\kslash+\poneslash)\gamma_\nu}{k^2(k+p_1)^2(k-p_2)^2}
    \Biggr) u_1.
\end{equation}
The integral can be solved by introducing Feynman parameters, as described in \cref{sec:phi4}, and shifting the integration momentum $k \to k-xp_1+yp_2$, resulting in
\begin{equation}
    i\M^i_v = -2ie^2Q_q^2\mu^{4-d} \eps^*_\mu(q) \int_0^1 dx \int_0^{1-x}dy \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{\widebar{v}_2 N^\mu u_1}{\bigl[ k^2+xy\shat \bigr]^3},
    \label{eq:M_virt_Nmu}
\end{equation}
where we have defined
\begin{equation}
    N^\mu \equiv \gamma^\nu \Bigl[ \kslash - x\poneslash - (1-y)\ptwoslash \Bigr] \gamma^\mu \Bigl[ \kslash + (1-x)\poneslash + y\ptwoslash \Bigr] \gamma_\nu.
\end{equation}
Using \cref{eq:gamma_contraction_3} and the Dirac equation for massless quarks, and keeping only terms where $k$ is an even power---the integral is antisymmetric for odd powers of $k$---the numerator simplifies to\footnote{The arrow signifies that this is not an equality in and of itself, but the reduction still holds when $N^\mu$ is integrated over, and placed between $\widebar{v}_2$ and $u_1$.}
\begin{equation}
    N^\mu \to \Biggl[ \frac{(d-2)^2}{d} k^2 + \shat \Bigl((2-d)xy + 2x + 2y - 2 \Bigr) \Biggr] \gamma^\mu.
\end{equation}
Inserting this back into \cref{eq:M_virt_Nmu} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    i\M^i_v &= -2ie^2Q_q^2\mu^{4-d} \widebar{v}_2 \gamma^\mu u_1 \eps^*_\mu(q)
    \\ &\qquad \times
    \int_0^1 dx \int_0^{1-x}dy \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{\frac{(d-2)^2}{d} k^2 + \shat \Bigl((2-d)xy + 2x + 2y - 2 \Bigr)}{\bigl[ k^2+xy\shat \bigr]^3}.
    \label{eq:M_virt_k}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Looking at the integral over $d^dk$, we see that only the $k^2$ term is \gls{uv} divergent, and we will regularize it with $\dUV=4-2\epsUV$, where $\epsUV>0$.
The remaining $\shat$ term is only \gls{ir} divergent, and is regularized by setting $\dIR=4-2\epsIR$, with $\epsIR<0$.
We recognize $\widebar{v}_2\gamma^\mu u_1\eps^*_\mu(q) = i\M^i_0$, and obtain the integrals over $d^dk$ from \cref{eq:general_loop_d_dim}, allowing us to write the amplitude as
\begin{equation}
    \M^i_v = \M^i_0 f(\shat),
\end{equation}
where\footnote{Outside of the brackets, $d$ should be understood as being either $\dUV$ or $\dIR$, so as to match the term it is being multiplied with.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    f(\shat) &= \frac{\alpha Q_q^2\mu^{4-d} (-\shat)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}}{2(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}
    \Gamma\biggl( \frac{4-d}{2} \biggr)
    \\ &\quad\!\! \times
    \int_0^1 dx \int_0^{1-x}dy
    (xy)^{\frac{d-6}{2}} \biggl[ (\dUV-2)^2xy + (4-\dIR) \Bigl( (2-\dIR)xy + 2x + 2y - 2 \Bigr) \biggr].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We can compute the spin- and color-averaged interference between the \gls{lo} and virtual contributions as
\begin{equation}
    2\overline{\Re\{\M^{i*}_0\M^i_v\}}
    = 2 \Re\{f(\shat)\} \overline{\abs{\M^i_0}^2},
\end{equation}
which inserted into \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar} gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_{\mathrm{v}}}{dQ^2} = 2 \Re\{f(\shat)\} \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}.
    \label{eq:sigma_virt_Re_f}
\end{equation}

The integrals over Feynman parameters in $f(\shat)$ are solved by first collecting powers of $y$, integrating over $y$, and then using the Euler beta function, given by~\cite{Schwartz:2014sze}
\begin{equation}
    \beta(a,b) = \frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)} = \int_0^1 dx (1-x)^{a-1}x^{b-1},
    \label{eq:beta_func}
\end{equation}
to solve the integral over $x$. This leaves us with
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    2f(\shat) &= \frac{\alpha Q_q^2\mu^{4-d}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}
    \biggl(-\frac{Q^2}{z}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \frac{\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma(d-3)}
    \Gamma\biggl(\frac{4-d}{2}\biggr) \Gamma\biggl(\frac{d}{2}\biggr)
    \\ &\qquad \times
    \Biggl\{
        \frac{1}{\dUV-3}
        + \frac{1}{\dIR-3}\Biggl( 1 + \frac{4}{\dIR-2} - \frac{6}{\dIR-2} \Biggr)
    \Biggr\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:f_after_xy_integral}
\end{equation}
where we have also substituted $\shat = Q^2/z$.

If we set $d=4-2\eps$, and expand around $\eps=0$, we find
\begin{align}
    2f(\shat) &= \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps) (-z)^\eps \Biggl\{
        -\frac{1}{\epsIR^2} - \frac{2}{\epsIR} + \frac{1}{2\epsUV} - 4 - \frac{\pi^2}{6}
    \Biggr\},
    \label{eq:fvirt}
\end{align}
where we have defined
\begin{equation}
    G(\eps) = \Biggl( \frac{4\pi\mu^2}{Q^2} \Biggr)^\eps \frac{\Gamma(1-\eps)}{\Gamma(1-2\eps)}
    = 1 + \biggl( \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2} + \ln(4\pi)-\gamma_E \biggr) \eps + \mathcal{O}(\eps^2).
\label{eq:G_eps}
\end{equation}
As shown in \cref{eq:sigma_virt_Re_f}, we only want the real part of $f(\shat)$. The only imaginary part comes from expanding $(-1)^\eps = 1 + i\pi\eps - \frac{\pi^2}{2} \eps^2 + \mathcal{O}(\eps^3)$. Taking the real part of \cref{eq:fvirt}, recognizing the \gls{lo} cross section from \cref{eq:sigma0i}, and setting $z=1$ (as allowed by the delta function), we end up with the following virtual contribution:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_{\mathrm{v}}}{dQ^2} = \hat\sigma^i_0 \delta(1-z) \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
    \Biggl\{
        -\frac{1}{\epsIR^2} - \frac{2}{\epsIR} + \frac{1}{2\epsUV} - 4 + \frac{\pi^2}{3}
    \Biggr\}.
    \label{eq:dsigmai_virt}
\end{equation}


\subsubsection{Counterterm Renormalization}

The \gls{uv} divergence in \cref{eq:dsigmai_virt} can be removed by renormalization, as described in \cref{sec:renormalization}. More concretely, we will do this by renormalizing the quark field. We define the quark's field strength renormalization $Z_q$ such that $\psi_q^0 = \sqrt{Z_q} \psi_q$, where $\psi_q^0$ and $\psi_q$ are the bare and renormalized quark fields, respectively; and rewrite the renormalization in terms of the counterterm $\delta_q$ such that $Z_q=1+\delta_q$.
We can then calculate $\delta_q$ in the on-shell scheme using \cref{eq:qed_ct_os_fermion}, which for the quark field gives
\begin{equation}
    \delta_q = -\frac{d}{d\pslash}\Sigma^q_2(\pslash) \biggr\vert_{\pslash = 0},
    \label{eq:deltaq_def}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma^q_2(\pslash)$ is the one-loop self-energy of the quark, and is given by
\begin{align}
    i\Sigma^q_2(\pslash) &= \inputtikz[baseline=(i.base)]{feynman_diagrams/initial_state/is_quark_self_energy}
    \nonumber \\
    &= 4\pi\alpha Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d} (d-2) \int_0^1 dx \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
    \frac{(1-x)\pslash}{\bigl[ k^2 + x(1-x) p^2\bigr]^2},
    \label{eq:Sigma2q}
\end{align}
where we have introduced the Feynman parameter $x$, and shifted the integration variable $k \to k-xp$.
Note that we use $p$ to denote the quark's momentum. This notation is restricted to the current calculation of the counterterm; it should not cause any confusion, as the combined quark-momentum---which also uses the notation $p$---does not appear in the present discussion.

Inserting \cref{eq:Sigma2q} into \cref{eq:deltaq_def} gives
\begin{equation}
    \delta_q = 4\pi i\alpha Q_q^2\mu^{4-d} (d-2) \underbrace{\int_0^1 dx(1-x)}_{=\frac{1}{2}} \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{1}{k^4}.
\end{equation}
The remaining integration over $d^dk$ is both \gls{uv} and \gls{ir} divergent, so after performing a Wick rotation and integrating out the angular parts, we will split the final Euclidean integral in two parts: $\int_0^\infty dk_E = \int_0^\Lambda dk_E + \int_\Lambda^\infty dk_E$, where $\Lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$ can be chosen arbitrarily, and will fall out of calculations.
The result after integrating is
\begin{equation}
    \delta_q = \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{4\pi} \biggl( \frac{1}{\epsIR} - \frac{1}{\epsUV} \biggr),
\end{equation}
which gives an amplitude of the form $\M^i_{\mathrm{c.t.}}=\delta_q\M^i_0$, where $\M^i_0$ is the \gls{lo} amplitude for $q\widebar{q}\to\gamma^*$ from \cref{eq:Mi_lo}.
The counterterm contribution to the full differential cross section then comes from the interference between $\M^i_{\mathrm{c.t.}}$ and $\M^i_0$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dsigmai_ct}
    2\Re\{ \delta_q \} \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2} = \hat\sigma^i_0 \delta(1-z) \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{2\pi} \biggl( \frac{1}{\epsIR} - \frac{1}{\epsUV} \biggr).
\end{equation}
Adding this to our virtual contribution in \cref{eq:dsigmai_virt} gives
\begin{align}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_{\mathrm{v+c.t.}}}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^i_0 \delta(1-z) \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
    \Biggl\{
        -\frac{1}{\epsIR^2} - \frac{2}{\epsIR} + \frac{1}{2\epsUV} - 4 + \frac{\pi^2}{3} + \frac{1}{2\epsIR} - \frac{1}{2\epsUV}
    \Biggr\}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \hat\sigma^i_0 \delta(1-z) \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
    \Biggl\{
        -\frac{1}{\epsIR^2} - \frac{3}{2\epsIR} - 4 + \frac{\pi^2}{3}
    \Biggr\}.
    \label{eq:dsigmai_virt_ct}
\end{align}
Thus the counterterm essentially converts the \gls{uv} divergence from the virtual contribution into an \gls{ir} divergence. Since $\epsUV=\epsIR$, nothing has changed numerically with our expression in \cref{eq:dsigmai_virt}.


\subsubsection{Real Photon Emission}

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.3\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz{feynman_diagrams/initial_state/is_emis_q} 
    \end{subfigure}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.3\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz{feynman_diagrams/initial_state/is_emis_qbar}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Initial-state Feynman diagrams for real photon emission.}
    \label{fig:is_emis}
\end{figure}

For the initial-state process involving real photon emission, $q\widebar{q} \to \gamma^*\gamma$, there are two contributions, one $t$- and one $u$-channel diagram. Their Feynman diagrams are shown in \cref{fig:is_emis}, and their matrix element yields
\begin{equation}
    i\M^i_r = eQ_q \mu^{\frac{4-d}{2}} \eps^*_\mu(q) \eps^*_\nu(k) \widebar{v}_2 S^{\mu\nu} u_1,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    S^{\mu\nu} = \frac{\gamma^\mu (\poneslash-\kslash) \gamma^\nu}{(p_1-k)^2} - \frac{\gamma^\nu (\ptwoslash-\kslash) \gamma^\mu}{(k-p_2)^2}.
\end{equation}

In addition to $\shat$, we define $\that$ and $\uhat$ to be
\begin{equation}
    \that \equiv (p_1-k)^2, \quad \uhat \equiv (p_2-k)^2,
    \label{eq:is_mandelstam}
\end{equation}
so that $\shat$, $\that$ and $\uhat$ act as Mandelstam variables for our initial-state partonic process.
The spin- and color-averaged square of $\M^i_r$ becomes:\footnote{Note that the interchange $(p_1 \leftrightarrow p_2)$ directly implies interchanging $(\that \leftrightarrow \uhat)$ as well. This follows directly from \cref{eq:is_mandelstam}.}
\begin{align}
    \overline{\abs{\M^i_r}^2} &= \frac{e^2 Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d}}{4\NC} g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}
    \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash S^{\mu\nu} \poneslash S^{\sigma\rho} \Bigr]
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
    &= \frac{e^2 Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d}}{4\NC} \Biggl\{
        \frac{1}{\that^2} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma^\nu\poneslash\gamma_\nu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma_\mu \Bigr]
        \\
        &\hspace{7em} - \frac{1}{\that\uhat} \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma^\nu\poneslash\gamma_\mu(\ptwoslash-\kslash)\gamma_\nu \Bigr]
        + (p_1 \leftrightarrow p_2)
    \Biggr\},
    \label{eq:Mir_bigtrace}
\end{split}
\end{align}
where, in the last equality, we have used the reversal property in \cref{eq:gamma_reverse} and the cyclic property of traces.

We can solve the Dirac traces using the relations in \cref{eq:gamma_contractions,eq:trace_gamma_2} to give
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma^\nu\poneslash\gamma_\nu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma_\mu \Bigr]
    &= 2(d-2)^2 \that\uhat,
    \\
    \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash\gamma^\mu(\poneslash-\kslash)\gamma^\nu\poneslash\gamma_\mu(\ptwoslash-\kslash)\gamma_\nu \Bigr]
    &= -2(d-2) \Bigl( 2\shat Q^2 + (d-4)\that\uhat \Bigr),
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
which inserted back into \cref{eq:Mir_bigtrace} gives
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\abs{\M^i_r}^2} = \frac{(d-2)}{2\NC} e^2 Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d}
    \Biggl\{ (d-2) \Biggl( \frac{\uhat}{\that} + \frac{\that}{\uhat} \Biggr) + 2(d-4) + \frac{4\shat Q^2}{\that\uhat} \Biggr\}.
\end{equation}

The initial-state partonic differential cross section was given in \cref{eq:dsigmai_scalar}. Using also the phase space we found in \cref{eq:dPi_2_k}, and recognizing the \gls{lo} cross section from \cref{eq:sigma0i}, we have
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i_r}{dQ^2}
    &=
    \hat\sigma^i_0
    \alpha Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d}
    \frac{\shat^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(1-z)^{d-3}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}} \Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}
    \\ &\qquad \times
    \int_0^1 dy \bigl[y(1-y)\bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \Biggl\{ (d-2) \Biggl( \frac{\uhat}{\that} + \frac{\that}{\uhat} \Biggr) + 2(d-4) + \frac{4\shat Q^2}{\that\uhat} \Biggr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmai_emis_yint_stu}
\end{align}
Inserting \cref{eq:momenta_quark_rf} into \cref{eq:is_mandelstam}, we can show that
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \that &= -\frac{Q^2}{z}(1-z)(1-y),
    \\
    \uhat &= -\frac{Q^2}{z}(1-z)y,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
which inserted into \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_yint_stu} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^i_r}{dQ^2}
    &=
    \hat\sigma^i_0
    \alpha Q_q^2 \mu^{4-d}
    \frac{\shat^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(1-z)^{d-3}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}} \Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}
    \\ &\quad \times
    \int_0^1 dy \bigl[y(1-y)\bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \Biggl\{ (d-2) \Biggl( \frac{y}{1-y} + \frac{1-y}{y} \Biggr) + 2(d-4) + \frac{4z}{(1-z)^2y(1-y)} \Biggr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmai_emis_yint}
\end{equation}

The integral over $y$ is solved using the Euler beta function in \cref{eq:beta_func}. Setting $d=4-2\eps$ we find\footnote{There are no \gls{uv} divergences for the real emission diagrams, so we can omit the subscript on $\eps$.}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_r}{dQ^2} = \hat\sigma_0 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps) 
    \Biggl\{
        \frac{-\frac{1}{\eps}+2 - 2\eps}{1-2\eps}(1-z)^{1-2\eps}z^\eps
        - \frac{2}{\eps} z^{1+\eps} (1-z)^{-1-2\eps}
    \Biggr\},
    \label{eq:dsigmai_emis_eps}
\end{equation}
where $G(\eps)$ was defined in \cref{eq:G_eps}. The factor $(1-z)^{-1-2\eps}$ can be expanded in $\eps$ using the plus distribution (see \cref{app:plus_dist}):
\begin{equation}
    (1-z)^{-1-2\eps} = -\frac{1}{2\eps} \delta(1-z) + \plusdist - 2\eps \plusdistln + \mathcal{O}(\eps^2).
    \label{eq:1-z_plusdist_expansion}
\end{equation}
Expanding also the other terms in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_eps}, we end up with
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i_r}{dQ^2} = \hat\sigma^i_0 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
    &\vastl\{
        \frac{1}{\eps^2} \delta(1-z)
        - \frac{1}{\eps} (1+z^2) \plusdist
        \\ &\qquad
        - \frac{(1+z^2) \ln z}{1-z}
        + 2(1+z^2) \plusdistln
    \vastr\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmai_emis}
\end{equation}
where we have also used that, for functions $g(z)$ such that $g(1)=0$, we have\footnote{Assuming, of course, that the expression is integrated over, or else the notion of plus distribution is nonsensical.}
\begin{equation}
    g(z) \frac{f(z)}{1-z} = g(z) \plusdistf.
    \label{eq:g_1-z_plusdist_relation}
\end{equation}

It is useful to see where the divergences in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis} originate. There are two types of divergences at play; a soft divergence, occurring when the energy of the emitted photon goes to zero, and a collinear divergence, which appears when the photon travels collinearly to one of the quarks. The former is captured by the limit $z\to1$, and the latter by $y\to0$ and $y\to1$, or equivalently: $\cos\theta\to\pm1$.
The collinear divergences come from integrating over $y$ in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_yint}, and are exposed in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_eps} as factors of $1/\eps$; while the soft divergence comes only from the expansion in \cref{eq:1-z_plusdist_expansion}, and is proportional to $\delta(1-z)$.\footnote{The $\delta(1-z)$ should not be surprising to see here, since it is only in the limit $z\to1$ that a soft divergence occurrs.}
The $1/\eps^2$ term in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis} contains both a soft and collinear divergence, stemming from the last term in the curly brackets of \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_eps}.
There are no soft $1/\eps$ terms surviving from \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis_eps}, since the remaining factor after expanding $z^{1+\eps}$ would be proportional to $\ln{z}$, which is zero in the soft limit.
Thus the remaining $1/\eps$ divergence in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis} is collinear.



\subsubsection{The Initial-State Correction}
The differential partonic cross section for initial-state corrections is now readily available by adding together the virtual (plus counterterm) correction in \cref{eq:dsigmai_virt_ct} and the real photon emission in \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis}. 
The resulting expression is then
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^i}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^i_0 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
    \vastl\{
        \Biggl(\frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4\Biggr)\delta(1-z)
        - \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z}
        \\ &\hspace{3em}
        + 2(1+z^2) \plusdistln
        - \frac{1}{\eps}\Biggl[ (1+z^2)\plusdist + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z) \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:dsigmai_nlo}
\end{equation}
The soft divergence from \cref{eq:dsigmai_emis} canceled against the virtual contribution, as expected, but a $1/\eps$ divergence still persists. We will postpone its treatment until \cref{ch:hadron_level}, where the hadronic cross section will be made finite by renormalizing the \glspl{pdf}.

It is worth noting the, perhaps unsurprising, relation between \cref{eq:dsigmai_nlo} and the corresponding \gls{qcd} correction at \gls{nlo}, which can be found in for example Ref.~\cite{Tore:2022}.
The \gls{qcd} correction is equal to \cref{eq:dsigmai_nlo} up to a change of coupling $\alpha Q_q^2 \leftrightarrow \alpha_s C_F$, where $\alpha_s$ is the strong coupling and $C_F=\frac{4}{3}$ is the quadratic Casimir of $SU(3)$ in the fundamental representation.

\subsection{Final-State Corrections}\label{sec:final_state}

We now turn to final-state corrections, for which we will use the final-state version of the differential cross section, as defined in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor}.
This means that we will need matrix elements for the process $\gamma^* \to \slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA}(+\gamma)$ appearing in \cref{eq:L_munu}, and all diagrams in this subsection should be understood as belonging to this process.
This is a $1\to2(3)$ decay, as opposed to the $2\to1(2)$ scattering processes considered for initial-state corrections, which somewhat complicates our calculations, requiring us to integrate over three-body phase space.
In addition, contrary to the fermionic quarks appearing in the initial-state corrections, the sleptons are scalars.
Therefore, the final-state corrections involve four-point interactions such as the $\slepton\slepton\gamma\gamma^*$ interaction in \cref{eq:eff_photon_rules}, increasing the number of contributing diagrams considerably.
The sleptons are also massive, and consequently the mathematical expressions will be more convoluted.
On the bright side there is no Dirac structure for the final-state corrections, as there are no fermions.

\subsubsection{Virtual Contribution}

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_triangle}
    \end{subfigure}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_bubble_B}
    \end{subfigure}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_bubble_A}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Feynman diagrams for virtual \acrshort{qed} corrections to the final-state.}
    \label{fig:fs_virt}
\end{figure}

There are three loop diagrams contributing to the \gls{nlo} correction to the final-state.
These are shown in \cref{fig:fs_virt}, and produce the following matrix element:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    i\M^f_v &=
    \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \eps_\mu(p) \frac{(2\pi\mu)^{4-d}}{i\pi^2}
    \int d^dk \Biggl\{
        \frac{k^\mu(k-k_1)\cdot(k+k_1+2k_2)}{(k^2-m_1^2)(k+k_1)^2\bigl[ (k+k_1+k_2)^2-m_2^2 \bigr]}
        \\ &\hspace{7.5em}
        +\frac{(k+k_1)^\mu}{(k^2-m_2^2)(k+k_2)^2}
        - \frac{(k-k_1)^\mu}{(k^2-m_1^2)(k+k_1)^2}
        + (\sim p^\mu)
    \Biggr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:Mf_virt}
\end{equation}
The terms proportional to $p^\mu$ will become zero when contracted with the initial-state part of the cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor} instead of $\eps_\mu(p)$, so we omit these terms in the remaining calculation.

\Cref{eq:Mf_virt} can be simplified using \cref{eq:pv_scalar_product}, and recognizing the resulting terms as the scalar and tensor Passarino--Veltman functions in \cref{eq:pv_def}.
Using \cref{eq:pv_tensor_reduction_B,eq:pv_tensor_reduction_C}, the tensor functions can be further reduced to scalar functions, and rewriting $k_2^\mu=p^\mu-k_1^\mu$, and omitting terms proportional to $p^\mu$, we end up with
\begin{equation}
    i\M^f_v = -
    \frac{\alpha}{\pi} g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2) k_1^\mu \eps_\mu(p),
\end{equation}
where we have collected the Passarino--Veltman functions inside the function $g$ defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2) &\equiv
    - (\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2) \Biggl[
        \frac{2\shat}{\lambda(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)} B_0(\shat;m_1^2,m_2^2)
        +C_0(m_1^2,m_2^2,\shat;m_1^2,0,m_2^2)
    \Biggr]
    \\ &\hspace{-4.5em}
    +\Biggl[
        \frac{1}{2}
        + \frac{2m_1^2(\shat+m_2^2-m_1^2)}{\lambda(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)} 
    \Biggr] B_0(m_1^2;0,m_1^2)
    + \Biggl[
        \frac{1}{2}
        + \frac{2m_2^2(\shat+m_1^2-m_2^2)}{\lambda(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)} 
    \Biggr] B_0(m_2^2;0,m_2^2),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
and we remind the reader that $\lambda$ is the K\"all\'en function given in \cref{eq:kallen}.
Furthermore, we can rewrite $k_1^\mu = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(p^\mu - (k_2-k_1)^\mu\bigr)$, and compute the interference of $\M^f_v$ with the \gls{lo} matrix element $\M^f_0$ for $\gamma^*\to\slepton^*\slepton$ from \cref{eq:Mf0}. After omitting terms proportional to $p^\mu$, and recognizing $(k_2-k_1)^\mu\eps_\mu(p)=i\M_0^f$ from \cref{eq:Mf0}, we find
\begin{align}
    2\Re\Bigl\{(i\M_0^f)^* i\M^f_v\Bigr\} &=
    \Re\biggl\{ (i\M_0^f)^* \frac{\alpha}{\pi} g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)
    \Bigl[(k_2-k_1)^\mu \eps_\mu(p) \Bigr] \biggr\}
    \nonumber \\
    &= \abs{\M^f_0}^2 \, \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \Re\bigl\{g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)\bigr\}.
\end{align}
The virtual correction to the full differential cross section for $q\widebar{q}\to\slepton^*\slepton$ is then
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_v}{dQ^2} = \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2} \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \Re \Bigl\{ g \bigl(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2 \bigr) \Bigr\}.
\end{equation}

The divergent terms from $g(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)$ are extracted in \texttt{Mathematica}~\cite{Mathematica} using \texttt{PackageX}~\cite{Patel:2015tea,Patel:2016fam}, giving
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_v}{dQ^2} = 
    \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \delta(1-z)
    &\vastl\{
        \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
        -\frac{1}{\barepsUV}
        \\ &\quad
        - \frac{1}{\barepsIR} \frac{\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)} \ln\Biggl[
            \frac{\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)}{2m_1m_2}
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_virt_dim}
\end{equation}
Note that we have extracted $\bareps$, not $\eps$, from the Passarino--Veltman functions, meaning that $g^{\mathrm{finite}}$ contains the finite part of $g$ as defined by the \gls{msbar} scheme.
This choice will allow us to cancel a few extra terms from the real emission contribution.

We can set $\shat=Q^2$ by exploiting the delta function, and use the dimensionless mass $\mt_i=m_i/Q$ to rewrite \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt_dim} as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_v}{dQ^2} =
    \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \delta(1-z)
    &\vastl\{
        \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
        -\frac{1}{\barepsUV}
        \\ &\quad
        - \frac{1}{\barepsIR} \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)} \ln\Biggl[
            \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)}{2\mt_1\mt_2}
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_virt}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Counterterm Renormalization}
We will remove the \gls{uv} divergence in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt} by renormalizing the slepton field, similar to what we did with the quark field in \cref{sec:initial_state}.
We define the slepton's field strength renormalization $Z_\slepton$, such that $\psi_\slepton^0=\sqrt{Z_\slepton}\psi_\slepton$, where $\psi_\slepton^0$ and $\psi_\slepton$ are the bare and renormalized slepton fields, respectively. Similar to the quark field renormalization, we can calculate the counterterm $\delta_\slepton$, defined by $Z_\slepton=1+\delta_\slepton$, in the on-shell scheme using \cref{eq:qed_ct_os_scalar}:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_\slepton = -\frac{d}{dp^2} \Sigma^\slepton_2(p^2) \biggr\vert_{p^2 = m^2}.
    \label{eq:deltal_def}
\end{equation}

The one-loop slepton self-energy $\Sigma^\slepton_2(p^2)$ is given by
\begin{align}
    i\Sigma^\slepton_2(p^2) &= \inputtikz[baseline=(i.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_slepton_self_energy}
    \nonumber \\
    &= -4\pi\alpha\mu^{4-d}
    \int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{(k+2p)^2}{k^2\bigl[ (k+p)^2-m^2 \bigr]}.
    \label{eq:slepton_self_energy_diagram}
\end{align}
Note that we use $p$ and $m$ to denote the slepton's momentum and mass, respectively. This notation is restricted to the current discussion of the counterterm; it should not cause any confusion, as the combined quark-momentum---which also uses the notation $p$---does not appear in the present discussion.

Expanding the numerator in \cref{eq:slepton_self_energy_diagram}, and using $2k\cdot p = (k+p)^2-m^2-k^2-p^2+m^2$, we can get rid of the loop momentum in the numerator:
\begin{equation}
    i\Sigma^\slepton_2(p^2) = -4\pi\alpha\mu^{4-d} \int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\Biggl\{
        \frac{1}{k^2-m^2}
        + \frac{2}{k^2}
        - \frac{2}{k^2-m^2}
        + \frac{2(p^2+m^2)}{k^2 \bigl[ (k+p)^2-m^2 \bigr]}
    \Biggr\}.
    \label{eq:slepton_self_energy_expanded}
\end{equation}
The first three terms in \cref{eq:slepton_self_energy_expanded} disappear when we differentiate with respect to $p^2$.
The remaining term is rewritten using Feynman parameterization, giving the counterterm
\begin{equation}
    \delta_\slepton = -4\pi i\alpha\mu^{4-d} \int_0^1 dx \int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{d}{dp^2} \frac{2(p^2+m^2)}{\bigl[k^2+(1-x)(xp^2-m^2)\bigr]^2} \Biggr\vert_{p^2=m^2}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating and setting $p^2=m^2$ leaves the following integral:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta_slepton_loop_int}
    \delta_\slepton = -8\pi i \alpha\mu^{4-d} \int_0^1 dx \int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \vastl\{ \frac{1}{\bigl[ k^2-(1-x)^2m^2 \bigr]^2} - \frac{4m^2x(1-x)}{\bigl[ k^2-(1-x)^2m^2 \bigr]^3} \vastr\}.
\end{equation}
Only the first term is \gls{uv} divergent, so we regularize it with $\dUV=4-2\epsUV$, while the second term is regularized with $\dIR=4-2\epsIR$, where we remind the reader that $\epsUV>0$ and $\epsIR<0$.
Using \cref{eq:general_loop_d_dim} to solve the loop integral in \cref{eq:delta_slepton_loop_int}, we find
\begin{align}
    \delta_\slepton &= \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \int_0^1 dx \biggl\{
        \biggl(\frac{4\pi\mu^2}{m^2}\biggr)^{\epsUV} (1-x)^{2\epsUV} \Gamma(\epsUV)
        + \biggl(\frac{4\pi\mu^2}{m^2}\biggr)^{\epsIR} \frac{2x}{(1-x)^{1+2\epsIR}} \Gamma(1+\epsIR)
    \biggr\}.
\end{align}
We can then expand the integrand in $\eps$ to give\footnote{For the factor $(1-x)^{-1-2\epsIR}$, this involves the use of the plus distribution relation in \cref{eq:1-z_plusdist_expansion}.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \delta_\slepton &= \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \Biggl\{
        \frac{1}{\barepsUV} + \cancel{\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}} + 2 \int_0^1 dx \, \ln(1-x)
        -\frac{1}{\barepsIR} - \cancel{\ln\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}} + 2 \int_0^1 dx\, x \biggl(\frac{1}{1-x}\biggr)_{\!\!+}
    \Biggr\},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where we use $\widebar\eps$ rather than $\eps$ to match \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt}, and have ignored terms that are $\mathcal{O}(\eps)$.

The two remaining integrals over $x$ are straight-forward to evaluate, using \cref{eq:plus_dist_int_def} for the plus distribution, and they end up canceling.
We are left with
\begin{equation}
    \delta_\slepton = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \Biggl( \frac{1}{\barepsUV} - \frac{1}{\barepsIR} \Biggr),
\end{equation}
whose contribution to the differential cross section comes from interference with the \gls{lo} amplitude---just as in \cref{eq:dsigmai_ct}---and is given by
\begin{equation}
    2\Re\{\delta_\slepton\} \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_{\mathrm{LO}}}{dQ^2}
    = \hat\sigma^f_0 \delta(1-z) \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \Biggl( \frac{1}{\barepsUV} - \frac{1}{\barepsIR} \Biggr).
\end{equation}
Adding the counterterm contribution to the virtual cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_{v+c.t.}}{dQ^2} =
    \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \delta(1-z)
    &\vastl\{
        \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
        -\frac{1}{\barepsIR}
        \\ &\quad
        - \frac{1}{\barepsIR} \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)} \ln\Biggl[
            \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)}{2\mt_1\mt_2}
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_virt_ct_dim}
\end{equation}
Just like in the initial-state case in \cref{eq:dsigmai_virt_ct}, the slepton field renormalization removes the \gls{uv} divergence, and replaces it with an \gls{ir} one.


\subsubsection{Real Photon Emission}

The final-state process for real photon emission is given by $\gamma^*\to\slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA}\gamma$.
There are three diagrams contributing to this process, which are shown in \cref{fig:fs_real}, and their matrix element is given by
\begin{equation}
    i\M^f_r = e\mu^{\frac{4-d}{2}}\eps_\mu(p)\eps_\rho^*(k) \Biggl\{
        \frac{(k_2-k_1-k)^\mu (k+2k_1)^\rho}{(k+k_1)^2-m_1^2}
        + \frac{(k_1-k_2-k)^\mu (k+2k_2)^\rho}{(k+k_2)^2-m_2^2}
        + 2g^{\mu\rho}
    \Biggr\},
\end{equation}
where $\eps_\mu(p)$ and $\eps_\rho^*(k)$ are the polarization vectors for the off-shell photon $\gamma^*$ and the emitted photon $\gamma$, respectively.
This can be simplified by expanding the denominators, rewriting the numerators in terms of $p^\mu$ by conservation of four-momentum, and using that $k^\rho\eps_\rho^*(k)=0$ for massless on-shell vector bosons, such as the emitted photon. We then find
\begin{equation}
    i\M^f_r = 2e\mu^{\frac{4-d}{2}}\eps_\mu(p)\eps_\rho^*(k) \Biggl\{
        \frac{(2k_2-p)^\mu k_1^\rho}{2k_1\cdot k}
        + \frac{(2k_1-p)^\mu k_2^\rho}{2k_2\cdot k}
        + g^{\mu\rho}
    \Biggr\}.
    \label{eq:Mf_emis}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_emis_B}
    \end{subfigure}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_emis_A}
    \end{subfigure}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.32\linewidth}
        \centering
        \inputtikz[baseline=(v.base)]{feynman_diagrams/final_state/fs_emis_4}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Final-state Feynman diagrams involving the emission of a real photon.}
    \label{fig:fs_real}
\end{figure}

As a consistency check, we can make sure the Ward identity holds for the emitted photon. Contracting the contents of the curly brackets in \cref{eq:Mf_emis} with the external photon momentum $k_\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
    k_2^\mu - \frac{p^\mu}{2} + k_1^\mu - \frac{p^\mu}{2} + k^\mu
    = 0,
\end{equation}
where we have inserted $p=k_1+k_2+k$.
Note in particular that the Ward identity would not hold if we did not include the four-point coupling leading to the right-most diagram in \cref{fig:fs_real}, showing that this diagram truly belongs to the same gauge invariant subset as the diagrams where the photon is emitted from one of the slepton-lines.

Taking the square of \cref{eq:Mf_emis} gives
\begin{equation}
    \abs{\M^f_r}^2 = -4e^2\mu^{4-d} \eps_\mu(p) \eps_\nu^*(p) \tilde{K}^{\mu\rho} \tilde{K}\indices{^\nu_\rho} + (\sim p^\mu,p^\nu),
    \label{eq:Mf_emis_squared}
\end{equation}
where we have defined the tensor
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{K}^{\mu\nu} \equiv \frac{k_1^\mu k_2^\nu}{k_2 \cdot k} + \frac{k_2^\mu k_1^\nu}{k_1 \cdot k} + g^{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
We can perform the $\tilde{K}$-contraction in \cref{eq:Mf_emis_squared} to give
\begin{align}
    K^{\mu\nu} &\equiv \tilde{K}^{\mu\rho} \tilde{K}\indices{^\nu_\rho}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &=
        \frac{m_2^2}{(k_2\cdot k)^2}k_1^\mu k_1^\nu
        + \frac{m_1^2}{(k_1\cdot k)^2}k_2^\mu k_2^\nu
        + g^{\mu\nu}
        \\ &\qquad
        + \Biggl[ \frac{k_1\cdot k_2}{(k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)} + \frac{1}{k_1\cdot k} + \frac{1}{k_2\cdot k} \Biggr] (k_1^\mu k_2^\nu + k_2^\mu k_1^\nu)
    \end{split}
    \nonumber \\
    &=
    \frac{m_2^2}{(k_2\cdot k)^2}k_1^\mu k_1^\nu
    + \frac{m_1^2}{(k_1\cdot k)^2}k_2^\mu k_2^\nu
    + g^{\mu\nu}
    + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2}{(k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)} (k_1^\mu k_2^\nu + k_2^\mu k_1^\nu),
\end{align}
where we in the last equality have rewritten $k_1\cdot k_2 = \frac{1}{2}(p^2-m_1^2-m_2^2) - p\cdot k$, and have simplified using conservation of four-momentum.
\Cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor} gives the differential cross section as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f_r}{dQ^2} = \frac{8\pi^2\alpha^2}{\NC\shat^3}\fqliab(\shat) \biggl(p_{1\mu} p_{2\nu} + p_{1\nu} p_{2\mu} - \frac{\shat}{2}g_{\mu\nu} \biggr) L^{\mu\nu},
    \label{eq:dsigmaf_tensor_restated}
\end{equation}
where $L^{\mu\nu}$ is given by inserting \cref{eq:Mf_emis_squared} into \cref{eq:L_munu}, giving
\begin{equation}
    L^{\mu\nu} = -16\pi\alpha\mu^{2(4-d)} \int d\Pi_L K^{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
% We have removed the terms proportional to $p^\mu$ or $p^\nu$ from \cref{eq:Mf_emis_squared}, since these disappear when contracted with the initial-state tensor in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor_restated}.\footnote{This is easy to see by explicit calculation, using $p=p_1+p_2$ with $p_1^2=p_2^2=0$ and $2p_1\cdot p_2 = \shat$.}
We have removed the terms proportional to $p^\mu$ or $p^\nu$ from \cref{eq:Mf_emis_squared}, since these disappear when contracted with the initial-state tensor in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor_restated}.
This is straight-forward to show using $p=p_1+p_2$ with $p_1^2=p_2^2=0$ and $\shat = 2p_1\cdot p_2$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:is_ward_proof}
    \biggl(p_{1\mu} p_{2\nu} + p_{1\nu} p_{2\mu} - \frac{\shat}{2}g_{\mu\nu} \biggr)p^\mu
    = \frac{\shat}{2} (p_1 + p_2 - p)_\nu
    = 0,
\end{equation}
and likewise for $p^\nu$.

By Lorentz covariance and the available tensors we know that $L^{\mu\nu}$ must have the form
\begin{equation}
    L^{\mu\nu} = L_1(\shat)\shat g^{\mu\nu} + L_2(\shat) p^\mu p^\nu.
    \label{eq:L_munu_emis}
\end{equation}
Contracting with $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $p_\mu p_\nu$ gives the coupled equations
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    g_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu} &= \shat \Bigl[ dL_1(\shat) + L_2(\shat) \Bigr],
    \\
    p_\mu p_\nu L^{\mu\nu} &= \shat^2 \Bigl[ L_1(\shat) + L_2(\shat) \Bigr],
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
which can be solved for $L_1(\shat)$, giving
\begin{equation}
    L_1(\shat) = -\frac{1}{\shat^2(d-1)} \Bigl[
        p_\mu p_\nu L^{\mu\nu}
        -\shat g_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu}
    \Bigr].
    \label{eq:L1_tensor}
\end{equation}
We could solve for $L_2$ as well, but since $p^\mu p^\nu$ gives zero when contracted with the initial-state tensor in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor_restated}---as shown in \cref{eq:is_ward_proof}---only the $L_1$ term will survive.

Writing out the contractions in \cref{eq:L1_tensor}, we get
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    L_1(\shat)
    &= \frac{16\pi\alpha\mu^{2(4-d)}}{\shat^2(d-1)}
    \int d\Pi_L \vastl\{
        \frac{\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2}{(k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)}(p\cdot k_1)(p\cdot k_2)
        \\ &\hspace{8em}
        +\frac{m_2^2}{(k_2\cdot k)^2}(p\cdot k_1)^2
        + \frac{m_1^2}{(k_1\cdot k)^2}(p\cdot k_2)^2
        + \shat
        \\ &\hspace{6em}
        - \shat \Biggl[
            \frac{m_1^2m_2^2}{(k_2\cdot k)^2}
            + \frac{m_1^2m_2^2}{(k_1\cdot k)^2}
            + d
            + \frac{\shat-m_1^2-m_2^2}{(k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)}(k_1\cdot k_2)
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:L1_scalar}
\end{equation}
Using four-momentum conservation we can rewrite the scalar products involving $p$ as
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    p \cdot k_1 &= \frac{\shat+m_1^2-m_2^2}{2} - k_2\cdot k,
    \\
    p \cdot k_2 &= \frac{\shat+m_2^2-m_1^2}{2} - k_1\cdot k,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
which after some tedious calculations, and remembering that the invariant slepton mass is defined by $Q^2=(k_1+k_2)^2$, reduces \cref{eq:L1_scalar} down to
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    L_1(\shat)
    &= \frac{16\pi\alpha\mu^{2(4-d)}}{\shat^2(d-1)}
    \int d\Pi_L \vastl\{
        \shat(2-d)
        - \frac{1}{2} \Bigl[\shat^2-(m_1^2-m_2^2)^2\Bigr] \Biggl[ \frac{1}{k_1\cdot k} + \frac{1}{k_2\cdot k} \Biggr]
        \\ &\hspace{8em}
        + \Biggl[
            \frac{1}{4}\Bigl(\shat-(m_1^2+m_2^2)\Bigr)^2
            -m_1^2m_2^2
        \Biggr] \Biggl[
            \frac{m_1^2}{(k_1\cdot k)^2}
            + \frac{m_2^2}{(k_2\cdot k)^2}
        \Biggr]
        \\ &\hspace{6em}
        + \frac{\bigl(\shat-(m_1^2+m_2^2)\bigr) \Bigl[ \frac{1}{4}\bigl(\shat+(m_1^2+m_2^2)\bigr)^2 - \frac{1}{2}(m_1^4+m_2^4+\shat Q^2) \Bigr]}{(k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)}
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Collecting all terms except the first onto a common divisor, the expression can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    L_1(\shat)
    &= \frac{16\pi\alpha\mu^{2(4-d)}}
    {\shat^2(d-1)} \int d\Pi_L \vastl\{
        \shat(2-d)
        \\ &\hspace{-2em}
        +\frac{\lambda(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)}{4(k_1\cdot k)^2(k_2\cdot k)^2}
        \Biggl[
            \frac{1}{2}(\shat-Q^2) \Bigl( m_1^2(k_2\cdot k)
            + m_2^2(k_1\cdot k) \Bigr)
            - Q^2 (k_1\cdot k)(k_2\cdot k)
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The remaining scalar products are calculated in the sleptons' \gls{rf} using \cref{eq:momenta_slepton_rf}, giving
\begin{align}
    k_i\cdot k &= \frac{\shat}{2Q}(1-z)
    \bigl(E_i^* \mp \kiabs\cos\theta^* \bigr)
    \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{\shat}{4}(1-z)
    \biggl[1\mp \Bigl(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)\cos\theta^* + \mt_2^2-\mt_1^2\Bigr) \biggr]
    \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{\shat}{4}(1-z)(1\mp x),
\end{align}
where $x$ was defined in \cref{eq:x_def}.
Using these scalar products, and rewriting the K\"all\'en function as $\lambda(\shat,m_1^2,m_2^2)=\shat^2\lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)$, $L_1(\shat)$ can be written as
\begin{equation}
    L_1(\shat)
    = \frac{16\pi\alpha\mu^{2(4-d)}}{\shat(d-1)}
    \int d\Pi_L \vastl\{
        (2-d)
        -\frac{4z \lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)}{(1-z)^2 (1+x)^2 (1-x)^2}
        (\xp-x)(x-\xm)
    \vastr\}.
    \label{eq:L1_dPiL}
\end{equation}

We are now ready to perform the phase space integral.
The phase space $d\Pi_L$ was defined by $d\Pi=dQ^2d\Pi_L$ in \cref{eq:dPi_L_def}, with $d\Pi$ given in \cref{eq:dPi_13}, resulting in
\begin{equation}
    d\Pi_L =
    \biggl(\frac{Q^2}{4\pi}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \biggl(\frac{\shat}{4\pi}\biggr)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \frac{(1-z)^{d-3}}{16\pi^3 \cdot 2^d \Gamma(d-2)}
    \Bigl[ (\xp-x)(x-\xm) \Bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    dx,
    \label{eq:dPi_L}
\end{equation}
with $\xpm$ given in \cref{eq:xpm}.
We then find
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    L_1(\shat)
    &= \frac{\alpha}{16\pi^2 \shat(d-1)}
    \biggl(\frac{4\pi\mu^2}{Q^2}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}}
    \biggl(\frac{16\pi\mu^2}{\shat}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}}
    \frac{(1-z)^{d-3}}{\Gamma(d-2)}
    \int_{\xm}^{\xp} \! dx \Bigl[ (\xp-x)(x-\xm) \Bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \\ &\hspace{9em} \times
    \vastl\{
        (2-d)
        -\frac{4z \lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)}{(1-z)^2 (1+x)^2 (1-x)^2}
        (\xp-x)(x-\xm)
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:L1_dx}
\end{equation}

The first term of the integrand is finite, and can immediately be integrated in $d=4$ dimensions to give
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \Bigl[ (\xp-x)(x-\xm) \Bigr]^{\frac{d-4}{2}} (2-d) \Biggr\vert_{d=4} = -2(\xp-\xm) = -2\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2),
\end{equation}
where the last equality follows directly from the definition of $\xpm$ in \cref{eq:xpm}.
We can insert $L^{\mu\nu}$ from \cref{eq:L_munu_emis} into the differential cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_tensor_restated}, and rewrite it in terms of the \gls{lo} cross section in \cref{eq:sigma0f} to give
\begin{align}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_before_xint}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f_r}{dQ^2} &=
    \hat\sigma^f_0
    \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \biggl(\frac{16\pi\mu^2}{\shat}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}}
    \frac{\lambda^{\frac{4-d}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)}{\Gamma(\frac{d-2}{2})}
    \vastl\{
        \frac{2(1-z)}{z \lambda(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)}
        \\ &\qquad
        + \frac{2 \lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)}{(1-z)^{5-d} \lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)}
        \int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \frac{\Bigl[(\xp-x)(x-\xm)\Bigr]^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}{(1+x)^2 (1-x)^2}
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\end{align}

It is worth noting that only the factor $(1-z)^{d-5}$ gives a divergence in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_before_xint}.
To see this, remember from \cref{eq:xpm_lim} that $-1 < \xm < \xp < 1$, meaning that the remaining integration over $x$ in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_before_xint} is finite---the only poles of the integrand are at $x=\pm1$, which are outside the integration domain.
To simplify the remaining $x$-integral, it is therefore useful to insert $d=4-2\eps$ already now, and expand the result around $\eps=0$.\footnote{Note that we omit the \gls{ir} subscript on $\eps$ since the real emission contribution has no \gls{uv} divergence.}
Rewriting \cref{eq:dsigmaf_before_xint} in terms of $\eps$, we find
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat{\sigma}^f_r}{dQ^2} &=
    \hat\sigma^f_0
    \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \biggl(\frac{16\pi\mu^2}{\shat}\biggr)^\eps
    \frac{\lambda^\eps}{\Gamma(1-\eps)}
    \\ &\qquad\times
    \vastl\{
        \frac{2(1-z)}{z \lambda}
        + \frac{2 \lambda_z}{\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}}
        \underbrace{(1-z)^{-1-2\eps} \int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \frac{\Bigl[(\xp-x)(x-\xm)\Bigr]^{1-\eps}}{(1+x)^2 (1-x)^2}}_{I^r}
    \vastr\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:_Ir}
\end{align}
where we have introduced the shorthands $\lambda\equiv\lambda(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)$ and $\lambda_z\equiv\lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)$ in order to declutter the notation.

We can now expand the integral around $\eps=0$, including also the divergent factor $(1-z)^{-1-2\eps}$ to make sure that we do not miss out on finite contributions from $\eps$-terms in the integrand. Using the plus distribution relation in \cref{eq:plus_dist_expansion}, we find
\begin{align}
    I^r &= (1-z)^{-1-2\eps} \int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \frac{\Bigl[(\xp-x)(x-\xm)\Bigr]^{1-\eps}}{(1+x)^2 (1-x)^2}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= \Biggl\{ -\frac{1}{2\eps}\delta(1-z) + \plusdist \Biggr\}
        \Biggl\{
            \overbrace{\int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \frac{(\xp-x)(x-\xm)}{(1+x)^2(1-x)^2}}^{I^r_1}
            \\ &\qquad
            - \eps \underbrace{\int_{\xm}^{\xp} dx \frac{(\xp-x)(x-\xm)}{(1+x)^2(1-x)^2} \ln\Bigl[ (\xp-x)(x-\xm) \Bigr]}_{I^r_2}
        \Biggr\} + \mathcal{O}(\eps)
    \end{split}
    \nonumber \\
    \begin{split}
        &= -\frac{1}{2\eps}\delta(1-z)I^r_1
        + \plusdist I^r_1
        + \frac{1}{2}\delta(1-z)I^r_2 + \mathcal{O}(\eps).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:Ir}
\end{align}
Inserting this back into \cref{eq:_Ir}, and expanding the remaining $\eps$-dependent factors we find
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f_r}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi}
    \vastl\{
        - \frac{\delta(1-z)}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl[
            \Biggl(
                \frac{1}{\widebar\eps}
                + \ln(4\lambda)
                + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2}
            \Biggr) I^r_1
            - I^r_2
        \Biggr]
        \\ &\qquad
        + \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda}
        + \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} \plusdist I^r_1
    \vastr\} + \mathcal{O}(\eps),
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_Ir12}
\end{equation}
where we used that $1/\Gamma(1-\eps)=1-\eps\gamma_E+\mathcal{O}(\eps^2)$.
Note that we have substituted $\eps \to \widebar\eps$ to absorb a few extra terms, as alluded to when making the same substitution in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt_dim}.

Instead of the rather complicated integral in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_before_xint}, we now have two simpler integrals to solve instead, namely $I^r_1$ and $I^r_2$ as defined in \cref{eq:Ir}.
They can be solved in \texttt{Mathematica} to give
% The integrals $I^r_1$ and $I^r_2$ defined in \cref{eq:Ir} are solved in Mathematica, giving
\begin{subequations}
\begin{equation}
    I^r_1 = -\bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \ln\Biggl[
        \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2-\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\mt_1\mt_2}
    \Biggr] - \sqrt{\lambda},
    \label{eq:Ir1}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        I^r_2 &=
        \bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr)
        \Biggl[
            \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1-\xp}{1-\xm} \Biggr) - \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm} \Biggr)
        \Biggr]
        \\ &\qquad
        -\sqrt{\lambda}\ln(4\lambda) + i\pi\bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \ln\Biggl( \frac{1+\xm}{1+\xp} \Biggr)
        \\ &\quad
        + \frac{1}{2}\bigl( \mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \ln\Biggl( \frac{1-(\xm)^2}{1-(\xp)^2} \Biggr)
        + \frac{1}{2}\ln\Biggl( \frac{(1-\xm)(1+\xp)}{(1+\xm)(1-\xp)} \Biggr)
        \\
        &+ \bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \Biggl[
            \ln^2(1-\xm) - \ln^2(1+\xm)
            + \ln^2(\mt_2^2) - \ln^2(\mt_1^2)
            - \ln(4)\ln\Biggl( \frac{\mt_1}{\mt_2} \Biggr)
        \Biggr],
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:Ir2_def}
\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
where $\Litwo$ is the dilogarithm, defined by
\begin{equation}
    \Litwo(z) \equiv -\int_0^z \frac{\ln(1-u)}{u} \, du,
    \label{eq:dilog}
\end{equation}
for $z\in\mathbb{C}$.

Finally, we can add the virtual cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_virt_ct_dim} to the real photon emission in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_Ir12}, and insert the expressions for $I^r_1$ and $I^r_2$ in \cref{eq:Ir1,eq:Ir2}.
The resulting differential cross section is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \frac{d\hat\sigma^f}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \vastl\{
        \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda}
        + \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} \plusdist I^r_1
        \\ &\qquad
        + \delta(1-z) \Biggl[
            \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
            - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
                \ln(4\lambda)
                + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2}
            \Biggr) I^r_1
            + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
        \Biggr]
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:dsigmaf_nlo}
\end{equation}
In contrast to the initial-state case in \cref{eq:dsigmai_nlo}, there are now no divergences remaining in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_nlo}.
The final-state corrections lack the collinear divergences that appear in the initial-state, due to the massive nature of sleptons. The soft divergence cancels with the virtual diagrams as normal, and we are left with a purely finite differential cross section.

\subsubsection{On the Realness of \texorpdfstring{$I^r_2$}{Ir2}}
At first glance, it might look like the cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_nlo} is not purely real, since there is an imaginary term in $I^r_2$ in \cref{eq:Ir2_def}.
This imaginary term, however, will cancel with an imaginary term coming from one of the dilogarithms.

The general dilogarithm $\Litwo(z)$ in \cref{eq:dilog} is complex for $z>1$.
It follows that $\Litwo\Bigl(\frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm}\Bigr)$ in \cref{eq:Ir2_def} is complex, since $\xm < \xp$.
We can extract its imaginary component using the following dilogarithm identity~\cite{Zagier:2007knq}:
\begin{equation}
    \Litwo(z) = -\frac{\pi^2}{6} - \frac{\ln^2(-z)}{2} - \Litwo\Biggl(\frac{1}{z}\Biggr).
\end{equation}
Using also that
\begin{equation}
    \ln^2(-z)=(\ln(z)+i\pi)^2=\ln^2(z) - \pi^2 + 2i\pi\ln(z),
\end{equation}
we can rewrite the complex-valued dilogarithm in \cref{eq:Ir2_def} as
\begin{equation}
    \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm} \Biggr)
    = \frac{\pi^2}{3}
    - \frac{1}{2}\ln^2\Biggl(\frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm}\Biggr)
    - \Litwo\Biggl(\frac{1+\xm}{1+\xp}\Biggr)
    - i\pi\ln\Biggl( \frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm} \Biggr).
    \label{eq:dilog_rewrite}
\end{equation}
The imaginary term then cancels the imaginary term in \cref{eq:Ir2_def}, and we are left with
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    I^r_2 &=
    \bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \Biggl[
        \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1-\xp}{1-\xm} \Biggr)
        + \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1+\xm}{1+\xp} \Biggr)
        - \frac{\pi^2}{3}
        + \frac{1}{2}\ln^2\Biggl( \frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm} \Biggr)
        \\ &\hspace{10em}
        + \ln^2(1-\xm) - \ln^2(1+\xm)
        + \ln(2\mt_1^2\mt_2^2) \ln\Biggl( \frac{\mt_2^2}{\mt_1^2} \Biggr)
    \Biggr]
    \\ &\qquad
    - \sqrt{\lambda}\ln(4\lambda)
    + \frac{1}{2}(\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2) \ln\Biggl( \frac{1-(\xm)^2}{1-(\xp)^2} \Biggr)
    + \frac{1}{2} \ln\Biggl( \frac{(1-\xm)(1+\xp)}{(1+\xm)(1-\xp)} \Biggr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:Ir2}
\end{equation}
which has no imaginary part.
Hence it is clear that the cross section in \cref{eq:dsigmaf_nlo} is also purely real.

\subsection{Non-Factorizable Corrections}\label{sec:non_factorizable}

We will now turn to \gls{nlo} corrections involving both the initial- and final-state of the \gls{lo} diagram in \cref{fig:M_lo}.
This includes interference between the initial- and final-state emission diagrams we encountered in \cref{sec:initial_state,sec:final_state}, and new loop diagrams with an added photon-line between the initial- and final-state particles.
The method of factoring the cross section that we introduced in \cref{sec:factorization}---and have exploited in \cref{sec:initial_state,sec:final_state}---breaks down for these corrections.
We therefore have to consider the full matrix element for $q\widebar{q} \to \slepton_{iB}^*\slepton_{iA}(+\gamma)$ in the calculations that follow.
This complicates the expressions significantly, and in principle one would have to deal with complications arising from dealing with the full phase space integration for a $2\to3$ scattering process.
Luckily, using some clever trickery, we will find that these non-factorizable corrections turn out to be surprisingly simple.

    \caption{Feynman diagrams for virtual corrections that are not factorizable. From top-left to bottom-right they are the triangle diagram, and the $t$- and $u$-style box diagrams, and the two vector bosons appearing in each diagram can be two photons or one photon and one $Z$~boson.}
    \label{fig:interference_diagrams}
\end{figure}

We start off with the virtual contributions, which we subdivide into two categories, the triangle and box diagrams, shown in \cref{fig:interference_diagrams}.\footnote{Sticking to pure \gls{qed} corrections, where only a photon is added to the \gls{lo} diagram in \cref{fig:M_lo}, we do not include the diagrams involving two $Z$~bosons.}
Consider first the triangle diagrams in \cref{fig:ifs_triangle}. The matrix element for the diagram with two photons is proportional to
\begin{equation}
    \int d^dk \frac{\widebar{v}_2  \gamma^\mu(\kslash+\poneslash)\gamma_\mu u_1}{k^2(k+p_1)^2(k+p_1+p_2)^2}
    = 2(2-d) \int_0^1 dx \int_0^{1-x} dy \int d^dk \frac{\widebar{v}_2 \kslash u_1}{\bigl[
        k^2 - y(x+y)\shat
    \bigr]^3},
    \label{eq:triangle_loop_integral}
\end{equation}
where we have introduced the Feynman parameters $x$ and $y$, removed $\poneslash$ and $\ptwoslash$ from the numerator by the Dirac equation, and shifted $k \to k-xp_1-y(p_1+p_2)$.
The resulting integrand is antisymmetric, and thus the integral is zero.
Changing one of the photons into a $Z$~boson adds a (complex) mass in the denominator, and a $\gamma^5$ to the numerator. These changes do not affect the argument made above, and the resulting integrals are also zero.

It should be noted, however, that this argument assumes massless quarks. If we include quark masses, the Dirac equation will not simply remove $\poneslash$ and $\ptwoslash$ in \cref{eq:triangle_loop_integral}, but give contributions proportional to the quark masses.
These contributions are, nevertheless, suppressed by the \gls{com} energy $\shat$ in the denominator, and are thus negligible.
We can therefore continue to work in the ultra-relativistic regime where the quark masses are set to zero, and consequently, the triangle diagrams in \cref{fig:ifs_triangle} do not contribute.

Next, we turn to the box diagrams in \cref{fig:ifs_box_t,fig:ifs_box_u}, which require a bit more care. We will denote the matrix elements by $i\M_{VV'}$ where the subscripts $V$ and $V'$ denote the upper and lower vector bosons in the box diagrams, respectively, i.e.\ such that
\begin{equation}
    i\M_{\gamma\gamma/\gamma Z/Z\gamma} =
    \inputtikz[baseline=(m.base)]{feynman_diagrams/interference/simple_box_t}
    + \inputtikz[baseline=(m.base)]{feynman_diagrams/interference/simple_box_u}.
\end{equation}
The matrix element for the diagrams with two photons is then\footnote{Recall that the photon does not mix the mass eigenstates of the outgoing sleptons, so $m_1=m_2$ in this case.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    i\M_{\gamma\gamma} &= i\alpha^2 Q_q^2 \delta^{AB} \mu^{4-d}
    \\ &\quad \times
    \vastl\{
        \frac{(2\pi\mu)^{4-d}}{i\pi^2} \int d^dk
        \frac{\widebar{v}_2(\kslash+\koneslash-\ktwoslash)(\kslash+\poneslash)(\kslash+2\koneslash)u_1}{k^2(k+p_1)^2(k+p_1+p_2)^2\bigl[(k+k_1)^2-m_1^2\bigr]}
        + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)
    \vastr\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We can simplify the numerator using Dirac algebra and the Dirac equation, and after recognizing the tensorial Passarino--Veltman functions in \cref{eq:pv_def} we get
\begin{equation}
% \begin{split}
%     i\M_{\gamma\gamma} &= i\alpha^2 Q_q^2 \delta^{AB} \mu^{4-d} \vastl\{
%         \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\rho u_1 g^{\mu\nu} D^{\gamma\gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho}
%         + 2\Bigl(
%             2\widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1 g^{\mu\nu} + p_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\nu u_1
%         \Bigr) D^{\gamma\gamma}_{\mu\nu}
%         \\ &\hspace{-1em}
%         + 4\Bigl(
%             2k_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2 \koneslash u_1 + (2p_1\cdot k_1 - m_1^2) \widebar{v}_2 \gamma^\mu u_1
%         \Bigr) D^{\gamma\gamma}_\mu
%         + 8(p_1\cdot k_1) \widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1 D^{\gamma\gamma}_0
%         + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)
%     \vastr\},
% \end{split}
\begin{split}
    i\M_{\gamma\gamma} &= -\frac{i\alpha^2 Q_q \zliab \mu^{4-d}}{s_W^2c_W^2} \biggl\{
        \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\rho u_1 g^{\mu\nu} D^{\gamma\gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho}
        + 2\Bigl(
            2\widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1 g^{\mu\nu} + p_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\nu u_1
        \Bigr) D^{\gamma\gamma}_{\mu\nu}
        % \\ &\hspace{-1em}
        \\ &\qquad
        + 4\Bigl(
            2k_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2 \koneslash u_1 - \that \widebar{v}_2 \gamma^\mu u_1
        \Bigr) D^{\gamma\gamma}_\mu
        - 4(\that-m_1^2) \widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1 D^{\gamma\gamma}_0
        + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)
    \biggr\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:M_box_photon}
\end{equation}
where the four-point Passarino--Veltman functions follow the notation from \cref{eq:pv_scalar_D}, and we have defined the Mandelstam variables\footnote{This definition differs from the initial-state case in \cref{eq:is_mandelstam}. In both cases it denotes the standard Mandelstam $\that$ for the relevant $2\to2$~scattering, so reusing the notation should not cause confusion.}
\begin{equation}
    \that \equiv (p_1-k_1)^2, \quad \uhat \equiv (p_2-k_1)^2,
    \label{eq:box_mandelstam}
\end{equation}
The arguments of the Passarino--Veltman functions are given by the superscript, as defined in \cref{tab:D_arguments}, where $\mu_Z^2 \equiv m_Z^2-im_Z\Gamma_Z$ is the $Z$-boson mass in the complex-mass scheme.

The matrix elements for the box diagrams containing one photon and one $Z$~boson are found in the same way as \cref{eq:M_box_photon}, resulting in
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    i\M_{\gamma Z/Z\gamma} &= -\frac{i\alpha^2 Q_q \zliab \mu^{4-d}}{s_W^2c_W^2} \biggl\{
        - 4(\that-m_1^2) \widebar{v}_2\koneslash\Gamma^5 u_1 D^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}_0
        \\ &\qquad
        + 2\Bigl(
            2\widebar{v}_2\koneslash\Gamma^5 u_1 g^{\mu\nu} + p_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\nu\Gamma^5 u_1
        \Bigr) D^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}_{\mu\nu}
        \\
        &+ 4\Bigl(
            2k_1^\mu \widebar{v}_2 \koneslash\Gamma^5 u_1 - \that \widebar{v}_2 \gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1
        \Bigr) D^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}_\mu
        \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\rho\Gamma^5 u_1 g^{\mu\nu} D^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho}
        + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)
    \biggr\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:M_box_photon_Z}
\end{equation}

The tensorial Passarino--Veltman functions appearing in \cref{eq:M_box_photon,eq:M_box_photon_Z} can be reduced to scalar functions using the expressions given in \cref{eq:pv_tensor_reduction_D}.
We can then recognize the \gls{lo} matrix elements, which are found from \cref{fig:M_lo}, given by
\begin{align}
    \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_\gamma &= \frac{8\pi\alpha Q_q \delta^{AB} \mu^{4-d}}{\shat} \widebar{v}_2 \koneslash u_1,
    \\
    \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_Z &= -\frac{8\pi\alpha\zliab \mu^{4-d}}{s_W^2c_W^2} \frac{1}{\shat-\mu_Z^2} \widebar{v}_2 \koneslash \Gamma^5 u_1,
    \label{eq:M_lo}
\end{align}
where we remind the reader of the shorthand $\Gamma^5=\zql(1-\gamma^5)+\zqr(1+\gamma^5)$.
After tensor reduction, the amplitudes for the box diagrams become
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \M_{\gamma\gamma} &= \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_\gamma \frac{\shat\alpha Q_q}{8\pi} \Delta^{\gamma\gamma},
    \\
    \M_{\gamma Z/Z\gamma} &= \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_Z \frac{(\shat-\mu_Z^2)\alpha Q_q}{8\pi} \Delta^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where we have defined
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \Delta &= \Bigl[
        (d+2)D_{003} + \shat(D_{123}+D_{223})
        - (\that-m_1^2)D_{133} - (\that+\uhat-2m_1^2)D_{233} + m_1^2D_{333}
    \Bigr]
    \\ &\qquad
    + 4 \Bigl[
        dD_{00} + \shat(D_{12}+D_{22})
        - (\that-m_1^2)D_{13} - (\that+\uhat-2m_1^2) D_{23} + m_1^2D_{33}
    \Bigr]
    \\ &\qquad\qquad
    + \Bigl[
        \shat D_{23} - (\that-m_1^2)D_{33}
    \Bigr] - 4\Bigl[
        (\that-m_1^2)D_1 + (\that+\uhat-2m_1^2)D_2 + 2m_1^2 D_3
    \Bigr]
    \\ &\qquad\qquad\qquad
    - 4 \that D_3 - 4(\that-m_1^2) D_0
    - (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
and the superscripts on $\Delta$ are passed on to the $D$-functions.
Note in particular here that we have changed the sign in front of $(k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)$. This was done by factoring out $\widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1$, which appears in all terms, and realizing that the interchange of $k_1$ and $k_2$ changes this to $\widebar{v}_2\ktwoslash u_1 = -\widebar{v}_2\koneslash u_1$, after applying the Dirac equation.

The interference between box and \gls{lo} amplitudes give
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:M_box_interference}
\begin{align}
    \overline{\M^{\mathrm{LO}*}\M_{\gamma\gamma}} &= \Bigl(
        \overline{\abs{\M^{\mathrm{LO}}_\gamma}^2} + \overline{\M^{\mathrm{LO}*}_Z \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_\gamma}
    \Bigr) \frac{\shat\alpha Q_q}{8\pi} \Delta^{\gamma\gamma},
    \\
    \overline{\M^{\mathrm{LO}*}\M_{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}} &= \Bigl(
        \overline{\M^{\mathrm{LO}*}_\gamma \M^{\mathrm{LO}}_Z} + \overline{\abs{\M^{\mathrm{LO}}_Z}^2}
    \Bigr) \frac{(\shat-\mu_Z^2)\alpha Q_q}{8\pi} \Delta^{\gamma Z/Z\gamma}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
When taking the spin- and color-averaged square of the \gls{lo} amplitude, and removing Dirac traces containing a single $\gamma^5$ matrix, we find that all the terms are proportional to $\Tr\bigl[ \ptwoslash\koneslash\poneslash\koneslash \bigr]$, which is symmetric under the interchange $(k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2)$.\footnote{Remember that $\koneslash=\poneslash+\ptwoslash-\ktwoslash$. Since $\poneslash^2=\ptwoslash^2=0$, we are left with only $-\ktwoslash$, and since there are two of them in the trace, the minus-signs cancel.}
Since $\Delta$ is antisymmetric under the interchange of $k_1$ and $k_2$, the box diagram interferences in \cref{eq:M_box_interference} are also antisymmetric.
Thus when integrating over the two-body phase space to get the box diagrams' cross section contribution, the integrand is antisymmetric under the interchange of integration variables $k_1$ and $k_2$, and we find---just as we did for the triangle diagrams---that the contribution from box diagrams is zero.

The non-factored Feynman diagrams for real photon emission are shown in \cref{fig:ifs_emis}, where \cref{fig:ifs_emis_q,fig:ifs_emis_qbar} show initial-state emission, and the final-state emission diagrams are displayed in \cref{fig:ifs_emis_B,fig:ifs_emis_A,fig:ifs_emis_4}.
We denote the matrix elements for initial- and final-state emission by $\M^q$ and $\M^\slepton$, respectively, and after some simplifications using conservation of four-momentum and the Dirac equation, we find
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    i\M^q &= \frac{1}{2}e^3Q_q\mu^{\frac{3(4-d)}{2}} (k_2-k_1)_\mu \eps_\nu^*(k)
    \Biggl\{
        \frac{Q_q\delta^{AB}}{Q^2} \widebar{v}_2 S^{\mu\nu} u_1
        - \frac{\zliab}{s_W^2c_W^2(Q^2-\mu_Z^2)} \widebar{v}_2 S^{\mu\nu} \Gamma^5 u_1
    \Biggr\},
    \\
    i\M^\slepton &= -2e^3\mu^{\frac{3(4-d)}{2}} \eps^{*\nu}(k) \Bigl( T_{\mu\nu} + g_{\mu\nu} \Bigr)
    \Biggl\{
        \frac{Q_q\delta^{AB}}{\shat} \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\mu u_1
        - \frac{\zliab}{s_W^2c_W^2(\shat-\mu_Z^2)} \widebar{v}_2\gamma^\mu\Gamma^5 u_1
    \Biggr\},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where
\begin{equation}
    S^{\mu\nu} \equiv \gamma^\mu \frac{\poneslash-\kslash}{p_1\cdot k} \gamma^\nu
    - \gamma^\nu \frac{\ptwoslash-\kslash}{p_2\cdot k} \gamma^\mu
    \quad \text{and} \quad
    T^{\mu\nu} \equiv \frac{k_2^\mu k_1^\nu}{k_1\cdot k} + (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2).
\end{equation}
We can show---remembering that Dirac traces with a single $\gamma^5$ matrix gives zero---that the interference between initial- and final-state emission is proportional to
% \footnote{The prefactor is not really relevant, so we only include the factor dependent on $k_i$ here.}
\begin{align}
    \overline{\M^q\M^{\slepton*}} &\propto (k_2-k_1)_\mu T_{\rho\nu} \Tr\Bigl[
        \ptwoslash S^{\mu\nu} \poneslash \gamma^\rho
    \Bigr]
    \nonumber \\
    &= \Biggl\{
        \frac{1}{(p_1\cdot k)(k_1\cdot k)}
        \Tr\Bigl[ \ptwoslash(\ktwoslash-\koneslash)(\poneslash-\kslash)\koneslash\poneslash\ktwoslash \Bigr]
        - (p_1 \leftrightarrow p_2)
    \Biggr\} - (k_1 \leftrightarrow k_2).
\end{align}
Thus $\overline{\M^q\M^{\slepton*}}$ is antisymmetric under the interchange of $k_1$ and $k_2$, while the three-body phase space, stated in its general form in \cref{eq:dPi_3_general}, is symmetric. When integrating over the phase space to acquire the cross section contribution, we therefore get zero, just as we did for the box diagrams.



\section{Summary}

In the preceding section we have calculated the partonic cross section for slepton pair production at \gls{nlo} in \gls{qed}. For the reader's convenience, we restate the important expressions here.
The \gls{nlo} corrections to the initial- and final-states are given by
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:summary_dsigma_nlo}
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^i}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^i_0 \frac{\alpha Q_q^2}{\pi} G(\eps)
        \vastl\{
            \Biggl(\frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4\Biggr)\delta(1-z)
            - \frac{(1+z^2)\ln z}{1-z}
            \\ &\hspace{3em}
            + 2(1+z^2) \plusdistln
            - \frac{1}{\eps}\Biggl[ (1+z^2)\plusdist + \frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z) \Biggr]
        \vastr\},
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:summary_dsigmai_nlo}
    \\
    \begin{split}
        \frac{d\hat\sigma^f}{dQ^2} &= \hat\sigma^f_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi} \vastl\{
            \frac{2(1-z)}{z\lambda}
            + \frac{2\lambda_z}{\lambda^{3/2}} \plusdist I^r_1
            \\ &\qquad
            + \delta(1-z) \Biggl[
                \Re\Bigl\{ g^{\mathrm{finite}}(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) \Bigr\}
                - \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} \Biggl(
                    \ln(4\lambda)
                    + \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2}
                \Biggr) I^r_1
                + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}} I^r_2
            \Biggr]
        \vastr\}.
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:summary_dsigmaf_nlo}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the \gls{lo} prefactors are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \hat{\sigma}^i_{0} &= \biggl\{ \frac{d-2}{2} \mu^{4-d} \biggr\}
    \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\shat Q^5}\fqliab(Q^2),
    \label{eq:summary_sigma0i}
    \\
    \hat{\sigma}^f_0 &= \Biggl\{
        \frac{3(d-2)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{d-2}{2}\bigr)}{2(d-1)\Gamma(d-2)}
        \biggl(\frac{4\pi \mu^2}{Q^2}\biggr)^{\frac{4-d}{2}} \lambda^{\frac{d-4}{2}}
    \Biggr\}
    \frac{8\pi\alpha^2 \kiabs^3}{3\NC\shat^3 Q}\fqliab(\shat),
    \label{eq:summary_sigma0f}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $\kiabs = \frac{Q}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \fqliab(Q^2) &= Q_q^2\delta^{AB}
    - \frac{2Q_q\delta^{AB}\zliab(\zql+\zqr)}{\sw^2\cw^2}\frac{Q^2(Q^2-m_Z^2)}{(Q^2-m_Z^2)^2+m_Z^2\Gamma_Z^2}
    \\ &\qquad
    + \frac{2\bigl(\zliab\bigr)^2(\zql^2+\zqr^2)}{\sw^4\cw^4}\frac{Q^4}{(Q^2-m_Z^2)^2+m_Z^2\Gamma_Z^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The initial-state $\eps$-dependent function $G(\eps)$ is given by
\begin{equation}
    G(\eps) = \Biggl( \frac{4\pi\mu^2}{Q^2} \Biggr)^\eps \frac{\Gamma(1-\eps)}{\Gamma(1-2\eps)}
    = 1 + \biggl( \ln\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2} + \ln(4\pi)-\gamma_E \biggr) \eps + \mathcal{O}(\eps^2),
\label{eq:summary_G_eps}
\end{equation}
the final-state virtual function $g$ is given by
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    & g(Q^2,m_1^2,m_2^2) =
    - (1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2) \Biggl[
        \frac{2}{\lambda} B_0(Q^2;m_1^2,m_2^2)
        + Q^2 C_0(m_1^2,m_2^2,Q^2;m_1^2,0,m_2^2)
    \Biggr]
    \\ &\;\;
    + \Biggl[
        \frac{1}{2}
        + \frac{2\mt_1^2(1+\mt_2^2-\mt_1^2)}{\lambda}
    \Biggr] B_0(m_1^2;0,m_1^2)
    + \Biggl[
        \frac{1}{2}
        + \frac{2\mt_2^2(1+\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2)}{\lambda}
    \Biggr] B_0(m_2^2;0,m_2^2),
\end{split}
\label{eq:summary_g}
\end{equation}
and the final-state emission integrals $I^r_1$ and $I^r_2$ are given by
\begin{align}
    I^r_1 &= -\bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \ln\Biggl[
        \frac{1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2-\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\mt_1\mt_2}
    \Biggr] - \sqrt{\lambda},
    \label{eq:summary_Ir1}
    \\
    \begin{split}
        I^r_2 &=
        \bigl( 1-\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2 \bigr) \Biggl[
            \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1-\xp}{1-\xm} \Biggr)
            + \Litwo\Biggl( \frac{1+\xm}{1+\xp} \Biggr)
            - \frac{\pi^2}{3}
            + \frac{1}{2}\ln^2\Biggl( \frac{1+\xp}{1+\xm} \Biggr)
            \\ &\hspace{10em}
            + \ln^2(1-\xm) - \ln^2(1+\xm)
            + \ln(2\mt_1^2\mt_2^2) \ln\Biggl( \frac{\mt_2^2}{\mt_1^2} \Biggr)
        \Biggr]
        \\ &\qquad
        - \sqrt{\lambda}\ln(4\lambda)
        + \frac{1}{2}(\mt_1^2-\mt_2^2) \ln\Biggl( \frac{1-(\xm)^2}{1-(\xp)^2} \Biggr)
        + \frac{1}{2} \ln\Biggl( \frac{(1-\xm)(1+\xp)}{(1+\xm)(1-\xp)} \Biggr).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:summary_Ir2}
\end{align}
The $x$ integration limits are given by
\begin{equation}
    \xpm = \mt_2^2 - \mt_1^2 \pm \sqrt{\lambda},
\end{equation}
and we have used the shorthands $\lambda=\lambda(1,\mt_1^2,\mt_2^2)$ and $\lambda_z=\lambda(1,z\mt_1^2,z\mt_2^2)$ for the K\"allen functions.
The remaining non-factorizable contribution was shown to be zero in the limit of massless quarks.
